# D164: One timestamped queue, and the few in front of the DJ have no order among them

Martin, 11-22 to 11-24, in the grilling of the Club Biad song-request prototype, correcting the lead in round 3 and settling the cost in round 4. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (rows 1.7, 1.20). Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520), feature set 6.

## Decided

- **One queue per venue per service day, ordered by when each request was asked,** adjusted only by popularity (D166). A request's place in it is a fact Restro knows.
- **The top N sit in an "awaiting play" set** — the DJ's working set. N is a venue setting, **3 by default** (D171).
- **Inside that set there is no order at all** (row 1.7), and the DJ has a **free choice** of which of its members to play next (row 1.20), because the third may beat-match out of what is playing when the second would not. "#2 before #3" is not a rule a DJ can keep. This is the single place beat matching needs, and it needs nothing else.
- **The set fills itself.** A request reaches it by being next in the queue, never by a DJ action. There is no "accept" gate.
- **Each card carries *played* and *didn't play*,** and the DJ taps one per song (row 1.20). **Nothing is ever marked as a side effect of something else.** *Played* is what captures the money (D163); *didn't play* is D167's refusal.
- **A mis-tap is undoable for about a minute,** restoring the set, the place and the cooldown, as the prototype's own undo does.
- **The DJ may mark *played* on any request in the queue, not only the few in his set** (settled by the lead after round 4). He often plays a track off his own laptop that someone has already paid for; without this the venue gives away exactly what it sold and the hold is released although the guest got his song.
- **A handover mid-night changes nothing:** the queue and every unmarked card stay where they are, and the handover itself is an audited event.

## Why

Martin corrected the lead's round-2 answer, which had replaced the queue with a time window, and the correction is what the rest of the design hangs on. Restro knows every request it holds, in order, timestamped, and which of them are in the working set — so the place is a fact. What Restro does not know is how long that place will take to arrive, because the DJ's own tracks play between ours and we cannot see his software. So the queue stays and the clock goes (D165).

The cost of the free choice is accepted openly: the system can no longer infer which song the DJ just played, so the play-mark cannot ride on anything else and has to be its own tap per card. The prototype earned its automatic marking only by forcing a strict order inside the set and letting the DJ play its head alone. **The choice is worth more than the tap.**

## Not decided here

The DJ's screen itself — its layout, its tap counts and its states — is a screen plan under D123, in the spec's feature set 6. Integration with DJ software or any player stays out of scope, as D99 had it.
