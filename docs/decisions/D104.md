# D104: Fable plans the heavy zone; "I don't know" gets a higher model's proposal

D58 sets the model that codes a ticket. This sets the model that plans: the grilling, the spec and the tickets. Martin 10-17. Why: planning is short next to building, but a wrong plan is the costliest mistake, and when Martin has no view, the recommendation he accepts becomes the decision. He wants no overkill on simple features and no mid-tier model deciding architecture.

**Planning tiers.** Sonnet plans by default. Fable plans the heavy zone: it runs the grilling, the spec and the tickets, sets each ticket's model by D58 (Opus for the core engine, Sonnet by default, Haiku for exactly specified work) and reviews the zone's diffs. Fable codes nothing; while the lead is on Fable, a ticket the lead would code itself (a nine-rule change, a migration, a guardrail) goes to an Opus session. Haiku never plans.

**The heavy zone** is a decision in any of:
1. Architecture: the data model, a schema change, a seam or connector, the boundaries between the three layers, a rule across packages, anything `wayfinder` charts.
2. Security: tenancy and RLS, roles, sign-in, tokens, secrets, outside input, payments.
3. Robustness: concurrency, retries, work failing halfway, sync and the absence rule, deletion and purge, money, time and the service day.
4. The nine rules, and work outside the current phase (ordering, payments, POS writes).
5. Any hard-to-reverse call Martin has no view on.

**Entering and leaving are announced.** The lead checks at classification and again during grilling. When a question crosses into the zone, it stops: "⚠ Heavy zone: <area>. Switch to Fable before we continue." Martin switches the model, or approves the lead switching it; nothing in the zone is decided on a lower model. When the zone's tickets are merged, the lead says the zone is closed and Sonnet can take over again.

**Outside the zone,** a Sonnet-planned feature set gets one read-only Fable check of its spec and tickets against the nine rules and the decisions in force, before the tickets are published.

**"I don't know."** When Martin has no answer to a grilling question:
1. If it waits on a fact, the fact is fetched first and the round goes on without it: public documents → a `research` agent; a person (Lukanet, a lawyer, a pilot owner) → a questionnaire Martin sends, and a line in `docs/open-items.md`; something to see → a `prototype`.
2. If it can be answered now, its complexity picks who thinks about it: easy to undo and one area → the lead, reasoning shown; a real trade-off outside the zone → an Opus agent; the heavy zone or hard to undo → a Fable agent at the highest effort.
3. The agent reads and never edits. It gets the question, Martin's answers so far and pointers to the decisions, research and code involved, and returns a proposal: its recommendation, the two or three options it weighed and why, what would change its mind, and how confident it is.
4. The lead puts the proposal to Martin as the next grilling question, worded so "yes" accepts it. Martin accepts or revises it; the agent never settles it.
