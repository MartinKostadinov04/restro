# D169: Paid greetings come in two kinds, are approved or refused with no revision loop, and settle on their song's marker

Martin, rounds 2 and 3 of the grilling of the Club Biad song-request prototype; he decided twice that greetings are in release 1. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (row 1.4). Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520), feature set 7.

## Decided

- **Greetings are in release 1,** at their own venue-set price, separate from the price of a request (D162, D171).
- **Two kinds, separately switchable by the venue:** one **tied to a song**, and one **free-standing**. A venue may want songs and tied greetings only.
- **A tied greeting is never a separate item.** It rides on its song's card: when that song comes up the DJ sees *read this first* with the text. One mark covers both (D163, D164). A tied greeting can never be read before its song — that is the whole point of tying it.
- **Free-standing greetings are one list, oldest first,** drawn as a tray with a count, not as a ranked queue, because places and times mean nothing for something read in batches. When the DJ picks up the microphone he opens it, reads one or several, and marks each done.
- **The guest is told the truth for each kind:** a tied greeting shows his song's place (D165); a free-standing one shows only *"waiting to be read"* and how many are ahead of it.
- **Approve or refuse, and nothing in between** (row 1.4). **The revision loop is dropped.** A refusal says "change the wording and send it again", and the guest edits and sends again from his own side. No DJ typing to a stranger in the middle of a set.
- **Refusing a greeting keeps the song.** Only the greeting's money is released (D167); the song's stays held and still plays.
- **Both kinds use the same marker and the same releases as songs** (D163): a greeting read into a microphone leaves no trace anywhere, which is exactly why it must never have a settlement rule of its own. "The greeting was not read" is one of the guest's report buttons (D163).
- **The DJ sees the text before it is read** (D167's condition), and the prototype's own length limit and control-character stripping come across as they are.

## Why

A DJ does not read greetings continuously — he picks up a microphone occasionally. Greetings are batched by nature, so they want a place that collects them, not a queue he works down track by track.

The cost of greetings in release 1 is recorded rather than re-argued: a second price, an approval step, a text a stranger reads aloud in a room, and the only outcome in the whole feature that nothing can observe. No product in the research sells them. Martin has decided twice that they are in, so they are in, and riding on the song's marker is what keeps them from needing rules of their own.

## Not decided here

The wording and the DJ's tray itself (D123, spec feature set 7).
