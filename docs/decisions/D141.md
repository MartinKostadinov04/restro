# D141: Who may do what in reservations, and the record of the night

Martin and the Port flow lead session, 11-06 and 11-07, with an independent Opus judgement on where a PIN is worth its friction. Sources as in D134; decision log Features 9 and 10, rounds 4 to 7. Their MVP gave reservations to hostesses only, as a job title, and kept its own separate history of changes.

## Martin's decisions

- **Working with reservations is a permission, not a job title** (10.21). Any role a venue makes can be given it, and no role has it because of its name. There is no reservations-only account.
- **Everyone signs in as himself** (10.1, 10.20): his own profile, like any other staff member. Leaving stops his access and his sessions.
- **A device remembers the person and keeps him signed in** (12.11): his session on that device stays active, and his permissions decide what he may do. On a device nobody owns, typing the PIN starts that person's own short session on it rather than authorising one action — so a run of record work is typed once. There is no second rule counting actions on top of the permissions.
- **Where a staff sign-in lands follows the person's role** (Martin, 11-08: "depends on the person's role"), never the device and never a choice made per person: the first screen his permissions allow, in the order Reservations (he may work tonight's reservations), Tonight (he may open and close tables), then the venue's page. Someone holding both lands on Reservations, because that is where a shift starts at the door. Nothing is configured for this; if a venue ever wants another home screen, it belongs on the role, with the rest of what a role carries.
- **Where the PIN falls** (12.9), Opus's judgement of 11-06, which Martin took in advance as the default and left **configurable in the role settings**. One rule decides it: *if the tap only says where people are sitting tonight, it is a tap; if it writes the booking book, touches money, touches a guest's details or ends the night, it is four digits.*
  - **One tap:** arrived or seated, seat a walk-in, change the guest count, add to the waiting list, give a waiting party a table, change a party's tables, swap two parties, free the tables, open or close a table from the plan, "New guests", mark a no-show.
  - **PIN:** create a booking, confirm or decline an online request, copy to another night, edit a booking's details, cancel, delete, restore, put back a reservation the night's end closed, set the pay rule, record a cash deposit, mark a prepayment taken at the till, start a refund follow-up, block or unblock a table, end the night (with its preview).
  - **His own sign-in, never a PIN:** erase a guest's details.
  - Why: tonight's floor is self-correcting and everyone is looking at the same plan, so a wrong tap is caught in seconds; a wrong line in the booking book is invisible for days and the club needs to know whose hand did it. The PIN is spent where it buys accountability, never where it buys a second of friction at the door.
  - Because it is configurable, what a PIN in the Log proves can differ between clubs. Martin, 11-07: that is the club's own choice.
- **Reservations write to the same Log as ordering** (9.20, D122 §9), readable by night and by reservation. There is no separate reservation history: one record of the night is the point of one platform.
- **A past night's plan is readable one month back** (9.21), showing the tables used and reserved then, each opening its reservation. Older nights stay readable as records in the Log.
- **What a change records** (9.1): who, when, the night, what changed and an optional reason — never a guest's contact details themselves (D140).

## The lead's calls

- **The permissions this adds**, each one a venue can give any role it makes: working tonight's reservations; managing bookings (edit, cancel, delete another's, restore); setting a reservation's pay rule; blocking a table; editing a night that has passed, where the venue switched that on (D135); ending the night; and the two settings-class ones, the reservation settings and the guest records. The settings-class ones need the person's own sign-in, which is why a PIN alone can never reach them.
- **A session on a shared device ends when the device has been idle a few minutes** (13.14), not after a fixed stretch: it is how a POS front end does it, and it never cuts someone off mid-task.
- **Each reservation action in the Log names its reservation**, so a night can be read either way round, and the Log's own retention covers it unchanged.
