# D139: Linked groups: one party across several tables, cumulated in Restro and nowhere else

Martin, 11-07, after two rounds on it. Sources as in D134; decision log 1.35, 1.41, 1.43, 1.60 (all replaced) and 12.23 to 12.29, 13.3, 13.13, 13.17. Built as its own ordering-stage ticket, [#319](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/319), in parallel with the hostess screen.

A party of twenty takes tables 111 to 114. Before this decision that was four tables with four bills and no total, and the design on paper was worse: it sent every phone round to one lead table's POS bill, which meant a round a waiter rang on table 113 was never read and never paid.

## Martin's decisions

- **The cumulation happens inside Restro only** (12.23). Every order still goes to the POS on the table it was made at, so the ticket that comes out names the real table and the POS records it there; payments settle on tables too. Restro holds the pointers and shows the bill and the settlement **grouped by table yet cumulative for the group**.
  - There is **no lead table and no common POS bill.** Every member table's bill is read as it is today, so nothing rung anywhere can go missing.
  - This replaces 1.41 and 1.60, and with them the amendment they had claimed on D117's one order per table and D122's sitting per table: both stand unchanged.
- **A group is made on the reservation in advance or from Tonight on the night**, and stays editable from Tonight while the party is sitting: tables added to it or taken out (12.24).
- **A group belongs to a stretch of one night, not to the tables** (12.25). The same tables can be a different group for a later booking the same date. It ends when the party's tables are freed or the night closes.
- **A guest at a grouped table sees the group's whole cumulative bill**, every table's lines included (12.28). That is the point of being grouped: the party asked for it, and a party that does not want it is not grouped — the hostess's act of grouping them is the consent.
- **Nothing on a bill is attributed to a person** (12.29). Restro never keeps which phone ordered which line, grouped or not, so a bill is tables and lines only. A guest who wants to cover part of it pays partially; there is no per-person tally to pay.

## The lead's calls

- **A reservation's tables are its group, always** (13.3). There is no separate grouping step for a booking on several tables, and a party that wants separate bills is booked as separate reservations. A per-venue default would leave a four-table booking ambiguously grouped.
- **A group is one thing, not two switches** (12.27). Tables in a group are booked together and reviewed together; nobody has to choose between a common reservation and a common bill, because the grouped-by-table format already settles table by table, so four couples on one booking can still pay separately.
- **Joining and leaving are pointer changes only** (12.26): a table joining a group that is already sitting keeps the lines it already has, and the group's total takes them in from that moment; taking a table out leaves its lines on it. With no common POS bill there is nothing to move.
- **Grouping from Tonight is quick on a shared device**, under the same permission as opening and closing a table; a phone at a grouped table pays its own table; **paying a whole group in one tap is the ordering side's own work** and not part of this (13.13, 12.18).
- **No new POS call.** A group is ours; the POS keeps seeing separate tables (D110, rule 7).
