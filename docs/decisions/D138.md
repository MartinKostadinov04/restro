# D138: Guests and regulars: whose list it is, what is kept, and what erasing means

Martin and the Port flow lead session, 11-06 and 11-07. Sources as in D132; decision log Feature 8, rounds 4 to 7. In their MVP each hostess kept her own private list of up to 2000 regulars that no colleague could see, and nothing was ever erased — how long details are kept was left to the owner and a lawyer.

## Martin's decisions

- **The list of regular clients belongs to the venue**, so every hostess sees it (8.20). A private list per hostess loses the club's regulars the day she leaves.
  - **The manager also has a private list**, by default, and the setting can change that.
- **Typing three letters of a name suggests a regular and fills in the phone** (8.2).
- **A guest's name, phone and email are kept while the venue uses Restro**; nothing is wiped on a timer (8.21). Martin, 11-06: "if you were a customer would you want your account name and phone number wiped out?"
- **A guest who asks to be forgotten is erased**, and his old reservations keep only the party and the table (8.21). The term, and the wording of the request, go to the lawyer.
- **A guest's card shows how many times he has come and how many times he did not**, counted from this venue's own nights only; nothing is shared between venues (8.22). It is what a hostess wants when a regular asks for the best table.
- **Those counts are for audits, not for accounting** (13.19, 13.20). Where a hostess forgot to mark an arrival, the night's end records a No-show that was not one, and that stays: the manager asks her what happened and that is the end of it. The counts tell a hostess roughly whether a regular turns up; they are never a record of money, and must not be turned into one.

## The lead's calls

- **A guest is a record of his own**, with its own prefixed id, keyed by a normalised phone number, so the same guest is one row however many bookings he has.
- **Erasing is an update that clears the details, not a deletion** (D83): the purge job's role covers a deleted venue's rows, and a single guest's erasure is ordinary service work that leaves the reservations standing with their party size and table.
- **Erasing also clears him from the venue's regulars and from the manager's private list, notes included** (13.15).
- **The lawyer's wording says that the payment provider keeps its own record of the card** (13.15): a guest who paid a prepayment is not erased from Stripe by us, and should not be told otherwise.
- **The Log never holds a guest's contact details before and after a change** (the 11-06 check): a Log entry names the reservation and that the details changed, never the old and new values, so erasing a guest cannot leave his phone number sitting in the record of the night.

## Open
