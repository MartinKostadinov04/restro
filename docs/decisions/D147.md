# D147: Staff mark an item sold out, and it is sold out for the whole club that night

Martin, 11-18, asked about 01:00 on a Saturday: the Jägermeister runs out, the POS cannot report stock, and D124 §6 forbade marking sold out on a POS venue. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-18-pos-support-answers-decision-log.md`.

## Martin's decision

In his words: "the moment that something is sold out a bartender or waitres or whoever is revieweing order can mark as sold out and this automatically brings it as sold out to everyone in the club for the night. If the item is paid it gets refunded and the session shows in their bill that it is refunded and out of stock. The out of stock would be automatic if the POS communicates properly inventory".

- **Anyone who reviews orders may mark an item sold out.** Not a manager's job: the bartender holding the empty bottle is the person who knows.
- **It applies to the whole venue for the rest of the service day,** not to one bar, one table or one order. It clears with the night.
- **Where the POS reports stock, Restro marks it sold out by itself,** with no tap.
- **A paid line of a sold-out item is refunded,** and the guest's bill says so. That half is [D148](D148.md).

## Why this does not break the rules

Rule 5 says our layers may only make the menu more cautious than the POS: we may hide or mark sold out, never un-hide, put back on sale or re-price. Marking sold out is the cautious direction, so it is allowed on a POS venue as much as on a hand-made one. D124 §6 forbade it for want of a decision, not for want of a rule; this is that decision.

## What this does not decide

- **Un-marking.** Nothing here says who may put an item back on sale, or whether the mark can be lifted before the night ends. A bartender who taps it by mistake needs a way back, and that is a screen question nobody has answered yet.
- **What a guest already holding the item in a bag sees.** The mark is venue-wide from the moment it is made; what happens to an unsent order containing it is unspecified.
- It says nothing about a POS that reports stock badly or late. Rule 6 still holds: absence is deletion only after a complete full read.
