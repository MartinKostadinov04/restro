# D98: Payments are a connector kind; the venue is the merchant; Restro never holds money

Payment providers plug into the platform like POS systems (Martin 10-17, grilling with the lead): one `PaymentProvider` interface in the platform, one package per provider, the platform knows no provider's name. The interface covers hold, capture, release, refund, a hosted checkout and status notifications, and each provider declares capability flags: can it hold and for how long, does it offer Apple Pay and Google Pay, can it split a charge. A provider that cannot hold runs song requests in a declared fallback: charge on request, refund on skip.

The venue is the merchant of record on its own payment account at the provider, one account per venue (the fiscal findings of 10-17 want each venue's money in its own account). Restro never holds funds. Restro's fee is invoiced to the venue (fixed, or a percentage computed from Restro's own records of captures), not split inside the payment, until a provider offers splits. The charging model itself is open (Q77).

The owner connects the venue's payment account in the venue's settings, next to Menu source: picks the provider, enters the provider's credentials, the platform checks them with a test call, and seals them like POS credentials.

Every capture records a receipt reference. Which payment types need a fiscal receipt from the venue's fiscal device, and under what regime, is the lawyer's answer (Q62–Q64, Q74, Q75); the money path is built so that answer can be applied without redesign.

myPOS is the first candidate provider (common in Bulgaria; holds, refunds and notifications exist in its checkout, see `docs/research/2026-10-17-mypos-and-fiscal-receipts.md`); whether Restro can integrate with it and the other providers venues use is researched before the interface is built (T80). Stripe is the second provider when a club or a market needs it.

**Open (10-17, same day):** the line naming myPOS first is a direction, not a choice. The provider is chosen after T80's research and the lawyer's answers to Q74 and Q75; no provider package is built before then. The business stance above (the venue is the merchant, Restro never holds money, the fee is invoiced) is firm.

**10-21 (#49, grilling in progress):** Martin chose myPOS as the first provider, which settles the direction above. The rest of the open line (connecting, the embedded form, the lead's technical choices) is settled in #49, and its log is `docs/deliverables/2026-10-21-payment-provider-decision-log.md`.
