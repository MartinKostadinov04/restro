# D148: If a payment went through Restro and staff mark the line undeliverable, Restro sends the money back

Martin, 11-18, after a Fable agent's proposal (`docs/deliverables/2026-11-18-sold-out-paid-line-refund.md`, design B) and after asking whether refunding through Stripe's API is safe for this use. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-18-pos-support-answers-decision-log.md`. This reverses the "Restro never refunds" rule that D125 3.5, D127 4.4 and 6.1, D132, D112, D122 §8 and D124 §4 were written around.

## Martin's decision

In his words: "I am not interested into imposing limits, if something cant be served due to a reason and the payument was passed to stripe the staff should be able to reverse it through our platform or through stripes dashboard. Now the usecase is out of stock, then we might find something different. Imo the rule is - if payment passes through our platform and due to any reason the staff puts as undeliverable, money are sent back upon the staff confirming the execution of the refund. And that is not a one time fix. we should apply this as part of our execution framework"

- **The rule, for any reason:** a staff member marks a paid line undeliverable, confirms, and the money goes back.
- **It is general.** Sold out is only the first case. This is how Restro behaves whenever something paid for cannot be served.
- **Two routes are both fine:** through Restro, or the venue's own Stripe dashboard.
- **No ceiling and no manager PIN** as a condition of refunding. Martin rejected the limits the proposal suggested.
- **Nothing ever blocks a refund, however bad the night.** Asked on 11-18 whether a night should ever reach a point where Restro stops offering the refund and sends staff to Stripe's dashboard, he answered: "No, never block". There is no cut-off at any size of night, and no night on which a confirmed refund has to be made somewhere else.

## The safeguards, which are the lead's to decide

These bound a mistake or a leaked key. They are not limits on what staff may do, which is what Martin rejected.

- Only a payment Restro itself captured, and only the amount the POS closed that line for.
- Once per line, with the refund row's id as the idempotency key, so a retry cannot pay twice.
- A separate refund key, used only by the worker, IP-restricted where the host allows it. It needs **"Charges and Refunds" Write in its Connect column** and nothing else (Stripe calls that permission `charge_write`), used with the `Stripe-Account` header — proven against the sandbox, and recorded in `docs/deliverables/2026-10-22-stripe-knowledge.md` (11-19, #435). Stripe has **no refund-only permission**: that same grant also allows capturing a charge, so the key is refund-only by use, not by Stripe's own limit. What narrows it is that the worker alone reads it and that only `refund-line.ts` may call `refunds.create`.
- An alarm and an automatic switch-off if Stripe reports a refund made with Restro's key that has no refund row.
- Every refund in the Log with the actor who confirmed it, and on the end-of-night page.
- **No local success (D72):** the guest's bill reads "refund being sent" until Stripe confirms, and only then "refunded".

## The POS side

A storno of a paid line is a new POS capability, under D110's "cancel within the POS's own rules". Where a connector declares it, Restro asks for the storno as a waiter would, before any money moves. Where it does not — every POS today — the existing storno to-do covers it.

## The nightly figure, which the lead set

Martin rejected limits on what staff may do, and that stands: no PIN, no per-refund ceiling, and no refund a staff member has confirmed is ever blocked. What the exposure needs is not a limit but a tripwire, because with no cap any bartender can send a guest's money back to a card with one tap and the Log is the only control after the fact. In a cash-heavy club that is a way to move money to a friend.

A threshold is the lead's to set, not Martin's. The industry benchmark is that refunds average about **0.13% of sales** ([Delaget's loss-prevention index](https://www.delaget.com/wp-content/uploads/formidable/2/Delaget-Restaurant-Loss-Prevention-Index.pdf)), and the practice the sources agree on is a figure measured against the venue's own baseline rather than one number for everybody, set high at first because a low threshold flags most of the staff on day one ([Mirus](https://www.fastcasual.com/blogs/to-catch-a-cash-thief-make-your-data-do-the-dirty-work); [Stripe on refund abuse](https://stripe.com/gb/resources/more/refund-abuse), which compares an account with its cohort rather than a cutoff). So:

- **Alarm** when a venue's refunds in one service night pass **1% of that night's captured amount** and number **three or more**. One per cent is roughly eight times the industry average, which keeps an ordinary night quiet.
- **The alarm never blocks anything** (Martin, 11-18: "No, never block"). It tells the owner, in the night's Log and on the end-of-night page. Every refund staff confirm goes through, however many there have been.
- **Recalibrated after the pilot's first month** against that venue's own baseline, as the sources advise. Until there is a baseline, the figure above applies.

The lead first set a second mark here as well: past 5 % of a night's takings or fifteen refunds, Restro would stop offering the in-app route and leave the venue its Stripe dashboard. A non-author review found that this is a limit on staff, which Martin had rejected — the sixteenth confirmed refund could not go through Restro, and a bartender at 01:00 has no dashboard. It was withdrawn before it shipped and put to Martin, who settled it: never. **No cut-off is to be built,** and the database holds the rule, not only the code: `payment_accounts.refund_route_off_reason` accepts one value, the stray-refund one below.

A second, non-negotiable switch-off stands apart from all of this and is not a limit on staff: if Stripe reports a refund made with Restro's key that has **no refund row**, the route stops at once and the owner is alarmed. That is a leaked key or a bug, not a bartender.

## What is open

1. **Fiscal law.** Whether the storno must precede the refund, and whether a storno may be issued through an API by Restro's POS user, is for the accountant and the lawyer.
2. Refunding on the venue's account needs the venue's written consent in our terms.

## What this does not decide

- Tips are untouched; a whole-payment refund stays in the venue's dashboard.
- A guest whose phone is closed is told by the waiter: Restro keeps no email or phone number (D125 3.7).
