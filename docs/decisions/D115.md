# D115: No table visit; the bill is the table's open POS bills, and a guest's order joins the table's POS bill like a waiter's

> **Refined by D124 (10-22):** a sent order never joins a POS bill opened before the current service day; it joins the oldest of this service day, else opens one where allowed, else "Ask your waiter".

> **Refined by D122 (10-21, the ordering check):** at a POS with one bill per table, a sent order goes onto the table's bill. A table takes phone orders only while staff have it open for phones in Restro.

> **Refined by D118 (10-20, #38):** an on-the-bill order is accepted only at an open table, one where a waiter has opened a POS bill, so by default only a pay-first order opens a new POS bill on an empty table. The location check is detailed there: when the phone asks, refusals, accuracy, the venue's spot and radius.

> **Refined by D117 (10-19, #37):** the bag is one per table, shared by every phone, so "a second phone's order waits a few seconds" becomes a second Send on the shared bag, whose order waits its turn as "Sending…". One order in flight per table is unchanged.

Martin 10-18, grilling "What is a table visit, and which bill does a new order join?" (#36 of map #29) with the lead (Opus) and two research subagents. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-10-18-table-visit-decision-log.md`. Sources, filed under D114: how other software decides a table visit (`2026-10-18-table-visit-in-other-software.md`), how QR ordering apps write orders into the POS (`2026-10-18-how-qr-apps-write-orders-into-pos.md`), the #35 decision log (`2026-10-18-bill-decision-log.md`), barsy's signed-in API reference (`2026-10-18-barsy-api-reference.md`) and the lead's signed-in re-reading of `Places_getlist` and `Accounts_getlist` on 10-18.

**There is no table visit.** No POS keeps one: barsy's table carries only the count of its open bills, and Toast, Lightspeed and Simphony likewise derive "occupied" from open checks. A waiter ties bills to people by the table and by memory. A closed bill belongs to no one in the waiter's work: it drops off the floor plan. Restro follows the POS ("all is in the POS"; "when a table is closed it's closed", Martin). A quiet-time rule and a visit worked out from opening and closing times were rejected: Restro would be inventing an ending the POS doesn't have.

- **The bill a table code shows is every open POS bill on that table:** what is owed, plus anything already paid against those bills. A POS bill that closes drops off the bill. That covers a pay-first order, a split someone paid and a bill a waiter closed. When everything is paid the bill is empty, and the next order simply goes in. A new group sees whatever the POS still has open on the table. Anything odd, such as a bill left open overnight, is staff's to sort out in the POS. This replaces D111's "everything ordered at a table during one visit" and D112's "a pay-first order appears inside the one bill as paid".
- **Nothing happens at 07:00.** The service day (D45) still stamps each order with its day, but it doesn't cut the bill.
- **The phone that paid remembers it.** A paid POS bill drops off the table's bill, so the phone that paid lists its own paid orders and receipt copies for the service day ("Your paid orders"), from Restro's own payment records (D111). How it looks is the bill screen's (#45).
- **A guest page left open when the table's POS bills close:** D111's knock shows "your bill changed", then the empty bill. Nothing is blocked.

**A guest's order joins the table's POS bill, like a waiter's.** Every QR ordering product that documents the point adds a second order to the table's open check (Toast with group ordering, Qerko, storekit on Lightspeed). None documents a new check per order as its design. Martin: "each guest opening their own order doesn't sound right". So an on-the-bill order is added as a round to the table's open POS bill, the oldest one when the table holds several (the one barsy keeps when staff merge, and the one Lightspeed returns). Only when the table has no open POS bill does the order open a new one, stamped with Restro's code. barsy refuses a repeat of that code, so that request may be resent unchanged for about a minute (D112). If the POS answers that the chosen POS bill has just been closed or locked, that is a definite "no": Restro reads the table again and uses the next open POS bill, or opens a new one. The platform only says "put this order on this table's bill"; each connector does it the safest way its POS allows. This replaces D112's "each guest order opens its own POS bill" (#35 log rows 4.1, 4.3 and 4.4). Unchanged from D112: a pay-first order is its own POS bill, paid and closed at once, and paying your own items is a split.

- **A round that times out is checked, never sent twice.** For about a minute Restro reads the table's open POS bills and looks for rows entered by Restro's own POS user (rule 9) after the order went out. If they are there, the phone shows "Added to your bill". If they are still missing after the minute, it shows D112's "We couldn't send your order, please call a waiter" and staff are alerted. Restro sends one order at a time per table, so the check can't confuse two orders; a second phone's order waits a few seconds. The cost is a few more "call a waiter" messages when the POS is slow, because Restro checks instead of resending. In return, a round is never doubled. One integrator documents the same choice (storekit never retries an add to an open bill).
- **This check is Restro's design, not a documented barsy feature.** barsy's row data carries who entered a row and when (`OrderData.user_name`, `date`), but nothing in its docs promises that a timed-out call is all-or-nothing or that a late row can't land after the minute. The check must be proven against barsy's test environment before ordering is built (`docs/connector-questions.md` C52). If Lukanet confirms Restro may set its own id on a row (Q82.3, C21), the check matches that id instead, which leaves no doubt. A POS with a documented guard on adding a round (Toast's unique row id, Simphony's request id, Square's key and version; C20) uses that guard.

**Other answers of the grilling.**
- **Location:** before ordering, the phone asks for the guest's location to check they are in the venue (Martin). No product researched does this. When it asks, what happens if the guest refuses, and the accuracy in a basement club are decided in "Guarding against fake orders on the bill" (#38). The location is used only for the check and never stored (D111).
- **No ordering from the bar:** what happens at the bar stays person to person until proven otherwise (Martin). This withdraws D111's "a venue that wants ordering at the bar puts table codes on the bar counter": table codes go on tables only.
- **Moving a bill to another table** stays a waiter's action in the POS (D112).
