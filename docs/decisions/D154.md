# D154: A bill move between tables is the staff's, at the POS, and a close carries our reference and nothing about the guest

The lead, 11-24, answering two of Mistral Software's WebAPI questions: "is moving or merging bills between tables possible?" (their 1.3) and "what data must we be given to issue the fiscal document?" (their 5.2). §1 was decided wrongly as a technical choice and Martin corrected it the same day: moving a bill is not ours to ask for, and D137 4.22 already said so. Their 26 questions, each mapped to the decision that answers it, are in the working file for that letter, kept outside the repository: `Documents/Restro/vendor-letters/` on Martin's machine (vendor correspondence stays out of a public repo, Martin 11-24).

## 1. Moving a party's bill to another table

**Withdrawn, 11-24, by Martin.** This section first said that where the POS declares a bill move, Restro asks the POS to move it. That was the lead over-reading D137 (4.22), whose subject is the staff: "staff pick the bill up in the POS and move it to the other table, through Tonight as a waiter would. Restro opens and closes the digital menus to match." The move is theirs; "through Tonight" describes where the hostess is standing, not who calls the API. D139 points the same way — joining and leaving a group are pointer changes, and "with no common POS bill there is nothing to move".

What stands:

- **Staff move a bill between tables, in the POS. Restro never asks for it** and adds no capability for it. Restro closes the old table's codes and opens the new table's, to match (D137 4.22).
- **Restro never merges two tables' bills,** in either direction, and no merge reaches the POS at all. A party across several tables is cumulated in Restro only, each table keeping its own bill and settling on its own (D139).
- **`moveLinesToTable` is unaffected:** moving the lines a guest is paying onto a payment table of our own is the payment route of D122 §8 and stays as it is. It is not a bill move between guest tables.
- **The end-of-night mismatch list** was the lead's reason for wanting the call, and it falls with it: if no bill is expected to follow a party, a bill still open on the old table is not a mismatch — the lines settle where they were rung, which is what D139 already says. If a real case shows the list needs more, it is a question for Martin with the case in hand, not a reason to write into a POS.

**Open, and the lead's to ask Mistral, not to assume:** when staff move a bill at the POS, what a reading then shows us — whether the bill is recognisable on the new table or reads as one bill gone and another appeared. The answer may need its own decision about how a reading is interpreted; it needs no write either way.

## 2. What a close carries, and what it never carries

D127 (3.10) needs a close Restro can recognise again in a later reading. That fixes the payload.

**Sent with the close:**
- the card payment type — a separate type such as "Card online", counted as card, where the venue keeps one (`closeWithPaymentType`);
- the amount, as the POS's own line amounts, plus the tip as its own amount where `recordTipOnClose` is declared (D127 3.2);
- **Restro's own reference for that payment,** always, where the POS keeps a field for it and returns it on a read. This is D127 3.10's third proof of a close. Where the POS has no such field, that proof route does not exist for it and the connector declares so: the close is then proven by a done answer, by `already_done`, or by the receipt number. The ordering build adds `ownCloseReference` for it;
- a short human-readable note — the real table's name and "Restro" — only where the POS shows or prints it, so the waiter carrying the paper receipt (D151) can see where it goes;
- the guest's email, only where `electronicReceipt` is declared and D122 §8's five conditions hold.

**Never sent to the POS:** the payment provider's own payment id, anything about the card, the guest's name, email outside the electronic-receipt case, phone, or payment handle, and nothing that says which phone paid. The provider's id has no use inside a POS, it would travel into the venue's database and could reach a printed document, and it already sits on Restro's payment record, which is where a dispute is answered (D122 §8). Nothing on a bill is attributed to a person (D139).

## What this does not decide

- **Corrections after a close** other than the undeliverable paid line of D148 — a wrong amount, a wrong payment type. They stay the venue's work, in the POS and in its provider dashboard, until a real case appears.
- **Whether a storno must come before the refund,** and whether Restro's POS user may issue a storno through an API. That is fiscal law, open in D148 and [#52](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/52).
- **Whether Mistral's WebAPI can do any of this.** The capabilities above are declarations, not assumptions: undeclared means not claimed (D122 §3).
