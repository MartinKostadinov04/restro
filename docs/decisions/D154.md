# D154: Restro moves a bill itself where the POS lets it, and a close carries our reference and nothing about the guest

The lead, 11-24, answering two of Mistral Software's WebAPI questions that no decision settled: "is moving or merging bills between tables possible?" (their 1.3) and "what data must we be given to issue the fiscal document?" (their 5.2). Both are technical choices, so they are the lead's, not Martin's. Mapped questions: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-24-mistral-webapi-questions-mapped.md`.

## 1. Moving a party's bill to another table

D137 (4.22) says moving a party that has already ordered is a bill move, done "through Tonight as a waiter would", and D110 names moving a bill as one of the things Restro may ask a POS for. Neither says which system makes the call.

- **Where the POS declares that it moves a bill to another table, Restro asks it to, from Tonight.** One call for the whole bill, never line by line: a half-moved bill is worse than none. On the POS's "done", the old table's codes close and the new table's codes open, as D137 already has it. On a refusal, nothing moved and the hostess is told so.
- **The move is refused before any call** when the destination table already has an open POS bill of this service day, because putting two parties' lines on one bill is the merge that D112 and D139 keep out, and when a payment on either table is not yet final (D122 §8: lines under a hold must not move out from underneath it).
- **Restro never merges two tables' bills,** in either direction. A party across several tables is cumulated in Restro only (D139).
- **Where the POS does not declare it,** staff move the bill in the POS, and Tonight carries a to-do: "Move the bill to table N in the POS now", with "Moved" to end it, under the permission that opens and closes tables.
- **While that to-do is open, the bill leaving the old table is not the everyday close.** D122 §4's closer — the bill gone from a complete reading — is suspended for that table until the reading shows the bill on the new one or a person answers. This is the point of the to-do: it is not a reminder for the staff, it is the fact Restro needs in order to read the next reading correctly.

**Why Restro makes the call where it can.** Without it, every bill move puts a false line on the end-of-night mismatch list (D122 §8). Restro's sent orders point at the old table and the old bill; a reading that shows that bill gone and a bill at the new table is, to Restro, a settled bill plus a stranger's. It cannot tell a move from a close. Doing the move itself, or being told one is coming, is the only way the night's records survive a party changing tables.

**A capability.** The ordering build adds `moveBillToTable` to the service capabilities, declared only where the POS moves a whole bill to another real table. `moveLinesToTable` stays what it is: lines onto a payment table of our own.

## 2. What a close carries, and what it never carries

D127 (3.10) needs a close Restro can recognise again in a later reading. That fixes the payload.

**Sent with the close:**
- the card payment type — a separate type such as "Card online", counted as card, where the venue keeps one (`closeWithPaymentType`);
- the amount, as the POS's own line amounts, plus the tip as its own amount where `recordTipOnClose` is declared (D127 3.2);
- **Restro's own reference for that payment,** always, where the POS keeps a field for it and returns it on a read. This is D127 3.10's third proof of a close. Where the POS has no such field, that proof route does not exist for it and the connector declares so: the close is then proven by a done answer, by `already_done`, or by the receipt number. The ordering build adds `ownCloseReference` for it;
- a short human-readable note — the real table's name and "Restro" — only where the POS shows or prints it, so the waiter carrying the paper receipt (D151) can see where it goes;
- the guest's email, only where `electronicReceipt` is declared and D122 §8's five conditions hold.

**Never sent to the POS:** the payment provider's own payment id, anything about the card, the guest's name, email outside the electronic-receipt case, phone, or payment handle, and nothing that says which phone paid. The provider's id has no use inside a POS, it would travel into the venue's database and could reach a printed document, and it already sits on Restro's payment record, which is where a dispute is answered (D122 §8). Nothing on a bill is attributed to a person (D139).

## What this does not decide

- **Corrections after a close** other than the undeliverable paid line of D148 — a wrong amount, a wrong payment type. They stay the venue's work, in the POS and in its provider dashboard, until a real case appears.
- **Whether a storno must come before the refund,** and whether Restro's POS user may issue a storno through an API. That is fiscal law, open in D148 and [#52](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/52).
- **Whether Mistral's WebAPI can do any of this.** The capabilities above are declarations, not assumptions: undeclared means not claimed (D122 §3).
