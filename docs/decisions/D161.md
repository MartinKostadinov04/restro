# D161: The admin is organised around the open venue

*Written as decision 154 on 11-18 and renumbered to D161 on 11-24. While this pull request waited for Martin, the ordering stage branch wrote its own 154 — a bill move between tables is the staff's, at the POS — so that number now belongs to a different decision. Nothing here changed with the number: D155–D158 keep theirs, and 159 is the Design panel's, on its own branch.*

Martin, 11-18: "Now everything is cluttered into tabs and subtabs, it feels unfinished and raw." Plan: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-18-navigation-and-feature-bases.md`, revised after two independent critiques beside it.

## Proposed

- On a venue's page **the sidebar is that venue's menu**, grouped: **Меню / Menu** (Studio, Tables & QR), **Поръчки / Ordering** (Tonight, its Settings), **Резервации / Reservations** (Reservations, its Settings), **Заведение / Venue** (Settings), then the organisation's Settings and the user menu as today.
- **The venue's name at the top of the sidebar switches venue**; inside it are the other venues, All venues and New venue. Off a venue's page the sidebar lists the venues, as today.
- **The tab bar on the venue's page goes.** The page's title names the screen; a page has at most one row of views, four at most.
- On a phone the same menu opens from ☰. A bottom bar for the service screens follows as its own ticket.
- A shared device (D122 §7) keeps its one row (Tonight, Reservations), with no sidebar, no venue list and no organisation switcher; its name in the top bar opens language, theme and sign-out.

## How Martin says yes

The lane 6 pull request builds this before Martin answers, so he can try it: **merging that pull request is his yes; a no closes it**, and today's tabs stay. For example: on Friday at 23:00 the manager opens Club Biad on her phone, taps ☰, then "Тази вечер" under Поръчки; next morning she taps the club's name at the top and picks Boulevard, landing on the same screen there.

## Why

Two levels instead of three: the sidebar says where, one row says which view. Every comparable back office checked (Lightspeed K-Series, Toast Web, Square) groups its left navigation by business area with a location switcher at the top; NN/g advises against mixing navigation tabs with in-page tabs. Sources are in the plan.

## Recommendation

Yes. It undoes Martin's 10-11 choice ("bring venues to the sidebar and keep the tabs"): then a venue had three tabs, now five tabs and twenty screens under them. Cost: switching venue is one tap longer.
