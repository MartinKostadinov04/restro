# D174: A song row is kept only while something refers to it and refreshed by its id before its thirty days are up; the imported catalogue is a later ticket

Martin, 11-25, on the recommendation of `docs/deliverables/2026-11-25-song-table-source-and-retention.md`, written the same day. [#522](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/522) was written to say that our song table "keeps songs permanently". It cannot: the provider's own policies cap stored data at thirty calendar days. The goal behind that sentence survives, and cheaply. Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520), feature set 2. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (rows 1.13, 1.23).

## Decided

**1. A song row is kept while something refers to it,** and not otherwise: a request at a venue, a live cooldown, tonight's queue, the DJ's history of the night. The row exists to serve a thing a guest did.

**2. A row a search merely returned, that no guest ever chose, is never kept.** The search shows its results and writes nothing. The writer is called when a guest asks for a song, with the details that search already returned.

**3. Those rows are refreshed by their id before thirty days,** by a worker job, through D173's one entry point — which is why that entry point owns refreshes as well as first sights.

**4. A row the provider stops returning is dropped,** not kept as a stale copy: the song was taken down or made private, and a request for it would fail in the room.

**5. Dropping is the purge job's,** through `restro_purge`, in the daily purge tick beside `purgeProviderNotices`. D173 point 6 already says this; the clock above is what gives it a schedule.

**6. Release 1 runs on the provider's search alone.** Row 1.23 fixed release 1's screening as the music-only search the MVP already does, and that is not reopened here. **The imported catalogue is [#567](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/567)**, a ticket of its own, with the Bulgarian long tail measured before anything is built.

**7. The quota extension request is filed early,** before the ceiling bites rather than after. It is Martin's to file: the form publishes no criteria and no timing, so the only control we have over it is how early it goes in.

## Why

### The thirty days are the provider's, in writing

A developer key with nobody signing in puts every search result in the provider's **Non-Authorized Data** bucket, and section III.E.4.d of its API Services Developer Policies caps that bucket:

> API Clients may temporarily store limited amounts of Non-Authorized Data for as long as is necessary for the purposes of the API Client but not longer than 30 calendar days. … after 30 calendar days, the API Client must either delete or refresh the stored data.

Checked rather than assumed: **there is no exception anywhere for identifiers.** A stored provider id is covered by the same clause as a stored title. The one longer-than-thirty-days allowance (III.E.4.b) is for Analytics and Reporting data, Authorized Data only, and says in its own words that statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data must not be kept beyond thirty days either. III.E.4.e adds a freshness duty on top. Source: https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies

### A refresh is not a search, which is what makes the clock affordable

The two calls come out of two separate buckets, and the difference is the whole reason #522's goal survives:

| call | bucket | cost |
|---|---|---|
| the search | **its own: about 100 calls a day, for the whole platform** | 1 per call |
| the details call | the general one: **10,000 units a day** | 1 unit, **up to 50 ids per call** |

So refreshing ten thousand stored songs costs about two hundred units a day out of ten thousand, and not one search. The clock buys a nightly worker job and about two per cent of the abundant bucket. Anyone who reads the thirty days as fatal to the table has conflated the two buckets.

### Why only what a guest chose

Two reasons, and they agree. The policy's own words are "temporarily store **limited amounts**", which a table of everything every search ever returned is not. And a result nobody chose buys nothing: what repeats in a club is the same hits being asked for again all night, by different people at different tables, and those are exactly the rows rule 1 keeps. The table still does what #522 wanted it for — with the budget spent, every song anyone has already asked for is findable from our own rows — it is simply smaller and honest about what it holds.

### Why release 1 does not change its source

The probe behind #567 is convincing: the open catalogue knows Bulgarian music (170 recordings for Преслава, 190 for Галена, chalga included), carries no thirty-day clock, and would remove the search ceiling instead of rationing it. But it is not a drop-in — the probe showed the hosted search cannot find a song the way a guest types it, so it means importing a catalogue and writing our own matching, transliteration and duplicate collapsing. That is a build, and row 1.23 is firm on what release 1 screens with. Shipping the cautious shape now costs a worker job; waiting for the catalogue costs the release.

### The one reading we deliberately did not take

III.E.4.d says "temporarily store limited amounts" and then "delete **or** refresh". Read literally, the second half permits an indefinite rolling refresh and sits badly with the first. This decision is built to the cautious reading, so nothing waits on an answer. Under Martin's standing rule — agents research plain legal rules, a lawyer only interprets — the literal reading is an interpretation question, and it is only worth asking if someone later wants rows kept that nothing refers to.

## What this changes in #522

The scope line "our own song table keeps songs permanently … a song seen once is searchable from our own rows for ever after" is replaced by points 1 to 4 above. Its acceptance test — with the budget spent, a guest can still find and request a song already in our table — stands as written. The rest of the ticket is untouched.

## Not decided here

- **Whether the imported catalogue is built at all,** and on what terms. #567 holds the licence and tier question, the long tail measurement, and Martin's go-ahead.
- **How the refresh job is scheduled** — a nightly tick or a sweep of rows nearing their day — which is the ticket's, under the worker's existing patterns.
