# D124: Stripe is the first payment provider; the pretend till stays for tables and bills

Martin 10-25, through the lead. Sources: Martin's messages of 10-25; #49's log `docs/deliverables/2026-10-21-payment-provider-decision-log.md` (rows 1.1 and 1.11); `docs/deliverables/2026-10-22-stripe-knowledge.md`; `docs/deliverables/2026-10-21-mypos-vs-stripe-cost.md`.

- **Stripe is the first payment provider Restro builds,** for the pilot. It replaces myPOS, which Martin chose on 10-21 (#49 row 1.1, D98's 10-21 line).
- **More providers follow later.** This is the first, not the only one (Martin 10-25). D98's shape stands: one `PaymentProvider` interface in the platform, one package per provider, and no provider's name in the platform. myPOS is parked, not dropped. Its facts stay in `2026-10-21-mypos-knowledge.md`, and its technical rows (#49 rows 3.1–3.3) are kept for when it is built.
- **Why it fits** (the lead's reading of the Stripe knowledge file):
  - Stripe holds a card paid with Apple Pay or Google Pay inside Restro's own page. So D122 §8's order (hold first, then move the lines, then capture) and the in-page form (#49 round 1, 6.6) both stand.
  - myPOS holds only typed cards, and only on its own page (#49 rows 1.7 and 1.9). That left rows 1.3 and 1.10 open.
  - Stripe's developer documentation and test mode are public.
- **The known trade-offs:**
  - On public prices, Stripe costs the venue about 0.47 points of card volume more than myPOS: about €280 a month at €60,000 (cost file).
  - Payouts take 3 business days, where myPOS's are instant.
- **What carries over unchanged:**
  - D98: the venue is the merchant on its own account, and Restro never holds money. Restro's fee is its subscription (D122 §9).
  - D122 §8: Restro's key creates, captures and cancels only, never refunds.
  - Tests run on the provider's test mode, never on a venue's live account (rule 7).
- **What #49 settles next, now on Stripe:** the Stripe equivalents of rows 3.1–3.3, and how a venue connects its account. The planning continues with #45, #53 and #61.
- **The pretend till stays** (`packages/connectors/sim-pos`, D122's simulated POS):
  - Stripe takes the money and knows nothing of tables or bills. Ordering and paying keep running against the pretend till until the pilot's till (Mistral, #48) is connected, and the pretend till stays for tests after that.
  - The simulated payment provider stays as the test double. The Stripe package is the real provider.
  - Going live with paying at the pilot club still needs the club's till connected, because a payment is matched against the club's actual bill.
