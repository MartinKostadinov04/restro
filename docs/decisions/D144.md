# D144: What the analytics answer, what the product already measures, and the one thing a night has to write down

The lead, 11-09, on Martin's instruction: "design the analytics based on the functionality". T84 asked Martin for the questions the analytics must answer; he handed that half back, so the questions below are derived from what the product does rather than asked of him. Every command, row and screen is something a club can be asked about, and a question only counts here if the product can actually answer it. Behind it: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-09-hospitality-analytics-research.md`, what OpenTable, Resy, Toast, SevenRooms, Resos, me&u and Flipdish actually report, each fact marked verified or not. D142 fixed the event model; this decision says what the events and the rows are *for*, and closes #356.

## The spine: Restro is not the till

Every money number a club acts on comes from its POS, which owns the bill, the price and the VAT (rules 5 and 7; the three layers). Restro must never become a second answer to "what did we take last night" — that is exactly the trap D142 names, two answers to one question. What the POS cannot see is **the phone, the door and the night's timing**, and that is what our analytics answer. Where a number of ours touches money it is a count of our own writes, never a statement of the venue's takings. Decision 140 on the reservations branch already set this tone for a guest's visit counts, "for audits, not for accounting"; it holds for every number here.

## The test every question passes

A question is answerable when the thing it asks about leaves a row, and that row keeps the **moment** and not only the final state. So each one lands in one of four places:

1. **In the rows** — a read. No new storage and no new event.
2. **Needs a field** — the row exists and keeps only its final state; a timestamp or a count on it.
3. **Needs an event** — it happens wholly on a guest's phone and leaves no row at all (D142's kinds).
4. **Not ours** — the POS's own, or something we deliberately never record.

Walking every kind in the Log, every service and reservation command, the eight reservation statuses and the nine event kinds through that test gives the finding this decision rests on: **the measurement is already complete.** The nine event kinds cover the phone; the reservation row stamps every moment at the door; the service rows stamp every moment of an order. Exactly one count is missing, and no new event kind is.

## What a night already answers, and for how long

`service_days.totals`, written once by `service_day.close` and never touched by the purge sweep: sittings, sent orders, of them added / not sent / unconfirmed, what reached the bill, waiter calls, alerts, pauses, payments, captured, tips, and the payments left unconfirmed with what they hold on guests' cards. Fourteen numbers a night, kept while the venue uses Restro.

Everything else expires. The Log at 31 days (`night`) or 18 months (`record`); sent orders and payments 18 months after their day ended or they settled; guest events 18 months. Reservations and guest records are kept while the venue uses Restro and are swept by nothing (decision 140, Martin: "nothing is wiped on a timer"), so the door's own rows do not expire.

**That is the whole design problem.** Not what to measure — the product measures it — but that in eighteen months nearly all of it is gone, and the fourteen numbers that remain know nothing of the phone, the door or the menu.

## 1. Did the night sell, and what sold?

| Question | Where it is |
|---|---|
| How much reached the bill through phones, and how many orders | In the totals, permanently |
| How many orders never reached it, and why | In the totals (not sent, unconfirmed); the reason per order in `sent_orders.reason` for 18 months |
| What sold, by item and quantity | In the rows: `sent_orders.lines` carries our node id, the quantity and the unit price. Nothing aggregates it, and in 18 months it goes |
| Which hour of the night sells | In the rows: `sent_orders.sent_at`. A send is the unit; we never need per-line times, because a phone sends a round |
| Which bar took it | In the rows: `sent_orders.bar_id` |
| Average per sitting, per table, per area | In the rows, by division |
| Margin, cost, VAT, discounts, stock, pour cost | **Not ours.** We never price and never read stock (rules 5 and 7). A club asking this is asking its POS |

## 2. Did the phone work?

The funnel is complete in D142's kinds: `menu.opened` → `item.opened` → `order.line_added` / `order.line_removed` → `order.send_attempted` → `pay.started` → `pay.finished`, plus `language.chosen` and `waiter.call_opened`.

| Question | Where it is |
|---|---|
| How many phones opened the menu, per night and per table | In the events |
| How many built a basket and never sent it | In the events: a sitting with `order.line_added` and no `order.send_attempted` |
| Scan to send: how many of the menus opened became an order, and how long it took | In the events |
| What gets looked at and never ordered | In the events: `item.opened` against the order's own lines |
| What gets put in and taken out again | In the events: `order.line_removed`. The only record of it — a line added and removed before a Send leaves no service row |
| Why a Send was refused | In the events: `order.send_attempted.outcome` |
| Bulgarian or English | In the events: `language.chosen` |
| Payments begun and not finished | In the events: `pay.started` against `pay.finished` |
| How many paying guests tipped (tip conversion) | **Needs a field.** The totals carry the tip money and the payment count, but not how many of those payments carried a tip, so a zero tip cannot be told from a tip never offered. One more count in the totals |
| Which phone, which guest, which device, who came back | **Never.** No device id, no identity across sittings, by construction (D142). A guest is only ever the table's sitting |

The service screens of the guest template (bill, bag, receipt) are in the face but not live (D71, D72). When the bill screen goes live, "opened the bill and did not pay" becomes a real step that leaves no row, and D142's own rule applies — a later question adds a kind to that file, with no migration. We add it then, not now: a kind for a screen nobody can open measures nothing.

## 3. Did the door work?

This is #356's answer. The reservation row stamps `created_at`, `starts_at`, `seated_at` and `ended_at`, and `ended_at` is set on every end state, so **every moment at the door is already in the row** and reservations emit no guest events at all. They are staff-side work; the staff side is the rows and the Log (D142), and a second answer is the thing we must not build. The one future exception is booking online (part 2, the `requested` status): a phone that opens the booking form and never sends it leaves no row, and that is a guest event when part 2 is built.

| Question | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Bookings, walk-ins and blocks for the night; covers booked | In the rows: `kind`, `party_size` |
| How many came, how many did not | In the rows: `status` |
| How far ahead people book | In the rows: `starts_at − created_at` |
| How late parties arrive | In the rows: `seated_at − starts_at` |
| How long a party sat | In the rows: `ended_at − seated_at` |
| How long a walk-in waited | In the rows: `seated_at − created_at` — a walk-in is created when it joins the queue |
| How long the queue was at any hour of the night | In the rows, reconstructed from each waiting party's join and leave |
| When a cancellation came, and how close to the hour | In the rows: `ended_at` against `starts_at` |
| Who took the booking, and through what | In the rows: `made_by`, `source`, `actor` |
| Prepayments asked for and taken | In the rows: `pay_rule`, `prepay_state`, `prepay_minor` |
| Whether a regular turns up | In the rows: the guest's own counts, which decision 140 fixes as an audit number and never an accounting one |
| Quoted wait against actual wait | **Not ours.** We quote nothing: the waiting list is a simple queue (decision 137). Toast reports this; we have no quote to compare against, by decision, and must not invent one |

**Three definitions are fixed here**, because the research found the trade disagrees and a club must not read a different number from ours than from its last system:

- **No-show rate** = no-shows ÷ (no-shows + parties that arrived), counting reservations and not covers, with walk-ins and blocks out of both sides and **Not seated out of both** — a walk-in cannot fail to arrive, and decision 138 says a party the night's end never seated is never a no-show. (Resos counts this way; OpenTable divides no-show covers by all covers, so the two are not comparable. Ours is the stricter and the more honest: it only compares parties that could have come.)
- **Covers** = the `party_size` of parties that arrived, blocks excluded. Cancelled, no-show and not-seated parties are not covers.
- **How long they sat** = `seated_at → ended_at`, named as itself and never as "turn time", because the trade has two incompatible ones (Resy seats to finish, Toast check to paid) and ours is the first. **A party the night's sweep ended is left out**: that measures a hostess forgetting, not a party leaving.

## 4. Did the venue cope?

| Question | Where it is |
|---|---|
| Waiter calls, alerts, pauses on the night | In the totals, permanently |
| How long a call waited for an answer | In the rows, 31 days |
| How long the POS took to answer a Send | In the Log, 31 days: a sent order's steps are its `sent_order.state` entries, and that kind is `night` |
| How long ordering was paused, and whose hand | In the rows and the Log, 31 days |
| Are we getting slower or faster, month on month | **Nowhere.** All of it is gone at 31 days |
| Which member of staff is quicker | **Open — Martin's call** (see below) |

## The decision: a night writes its own numbers down

Everything above is a read, except that most of what it reads expires. So:

**`service_day.close` writes the night's numbers, and it is the only thing that does.** The numbers of a night are a fact of that night; they are computed once, when the night ends, from rows that are all still there, and kept while the venue uses Restro. Nothing recomputes a night later from rows that have since been swept, because that would quietly give a different answer each time it ran.

- **The scalars go in `service_days.totals`**, beside the fourteen already there: the funnel's counts for the night (menus opened, baskets built, sends tried and refused, payments begun and finished, calls, languages), the door's counts (bookings, walk-ins, arrived, no-shows, cancelled, not seated, covers booked and seated, prepayments), the night's timings as medians (how long parties sat, how late they arrived, how long walk-ins waited, how long the POS took), and the missing tip count. It is a jsonb object of scalars, so this needs no migration — the same property D142 gives a new event kind.
- **What sold goes in its own table**, one row per item per night (our node id, the quantity, what it added to bills), because that list grows with the menu and has no business inside a single row of scalars. Written at close, swept by nothing.
- **A night that is written again is written whole.** Reservations may be edited after the night where a venue allows it (decision 135), so a write that touches a closed night re-rolls that night's numbers rather than patching them. Reservation rows do not expire, so a re-roll is always possible; an order or a funnel number past 18 months is not re-rollable and is never recomputed.
- **Reading them follows the night's own permissions.** The counts are `night.view`; only what names money stays `night.view_full`, as the tips already do.

**What this decision does not cover:** where the numbers appear. The screens — the owner's month, a comparison between nights, whatever Martin wants on the venue's page — are a Build with their own spec, and D107's rules for operational screens apply to them. This decision only settles the questions, the definitions, and that the night writes its numbers down before the rows go.

## Martin's decision: keep to the Log

**No number per member of staff.** Everything needed is already recorded — the Log names who did each thing, and decision 141 puts a PIN where a club wants accountability — so "which hostess seats a party fastest", "who answers calls quickest" and "whose tables tip most" are all derivable today. Martin, 11-09, on the lead's recommendation: **keep to the Log.**

So: a manager reads who did what on a night, which is what accountability actually needs, and **no screen ranks people against each other** — not for a night, not for a season. The reason is not sentiment: a ranking built on taps changes how staff behave at the door, they stop marking the awkward things, and that corrupts the very rows every other number in this decision depends on. A number that makes its own inputs worse is not a measurement.

What this forbids is a surface, not a recording. The Log keeps naming the actor on every entry, as D122 §9 requires, and nothing here changes what is stored. A screen, a report, a CSV column or an export that puts staff side by side on any of these numbers is out, and a ticket asking for one is a stop-and-ask against this decision.
