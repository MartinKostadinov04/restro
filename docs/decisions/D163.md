# D163: The guest's money is held when he asks and taken only when the DJ marks the song played

Martin, 11-22 to 11-24, in the grilling of the Club Biad song-request prototype. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (rows 1.6, 1.15, 1.18, 1.22, 1.25). Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520), feature set 3. Built on D125, D126 and D127 (the payment provider and its card form) and D120 (the venue Log), which stand. This is the promise the whole feature rests on: a guest hands over the price of a request for something that may not happen.

## Decided

- **A hold at the ask.** The card is authorised, not charged, through the venue's own payment connection, with the card authenticated as D127 requires. No card is captured at the ask.
- **Capture when the DJ marks the song played** (row 1.6) — the closest trigger to the real event that is available to us. Nothing else captures. A capture without a play-mark is not a path that exists (rule 5's caution, carried into money).
- **Three releases, and no fourth:**
  - **the DJ refuses it** (D167): released at once;
  - **ninety minutes pass without a play** (row 1.22): released by itself, not only at the night's end;
  - **the night closes** with the request neither played nor refused: released by the night's sweep.
- **A release is a release, never a refund** (row 1.18). No fee, no refund key, its own Log entry and its own words to the guest. Money that was never taken is not being given back.
- **The floor is deliberately generous.** Anything still unmarked when the night closes with nobody sweeping it is **released, not captured**. The venue loses a sale rather than a guest paying for silence. Martin: "worst case scenario is due to DJ mistake the club lost a few bugs — what is important is that our decisions are reasonable and robust."
- **A guest may take his request back while it is still waiting** (row 1.15), not once it has reached the DJ's awaiting-play set (D164). Withdrawing releases his hold.
- **The guest is told the rule in plain words all the way through the flow** (row 1.22): you do not pay until the DJ plays your song or reads your greeting; the money is frozen until then; if ninety minutes pass without it, we release it. Short and friendly, where he decides and not in small print, on the waiting screen, and again in the release message. The exact words and where they appear are the screen plan's (D123).
- **No card is kept** (row 1.25). Martin: "I think it's a security risk to keep credit card details." Apple Pay and Google Pay are the fast path for a repeat request; a raw card is entered again each time. Recorded and not argued: a saved card would live at the provider and never on our servers, and a repeat may still be challenged by the bank, so the gain was smaller than it sounded.

## The guest whose song never played (row 1.18)

- **Nobody has to answer him for the money.** The release is automatic, by the rules above.
- **He can file a report in a few taps** — a short fixed list of what went wrong, no free typing required, filed against his own request. It notifies staff and lands in the venue Log (D120) as the dispute's record.
- **No published response time.** Nothing promises when someone will answer.
- A guest who lost his phone, and with it his session (D165), takes decision 148's staff route.

## Why

Martin's worry was the right one, and it has no free answer: someone has to absorb a forgotten tap. Capture on the play-mark and a forgotten tap costs the venue a sale; capture on acceptance and a forgotten tap means a guest paid for silence, which is the complaint the market leader is known for. So the loss is put on the venue on purpose, and the tap is taken off the critical path instead (D164): one card in flight, two large controls, a nudge when something has sat unmarked, an end-of-night sweep that clears whatever is left before the night's totals are read.

The ninety-minute release is what keeps the DJ's open door honest (D168): nobody waits all night on a request that will not arrive.

## Not decided here

Partial capture, chip-ins and succession (D162: the structure stays open, none is built). The provider's own authorisation lifetime, which the ticket reconciles against; if it is ever documented as shorter than a club night, the ninety minutes stop being a courtesy and become a constraint, and that is a stop-and-ask.
