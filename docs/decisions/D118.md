# D118: Guards against fake orders on the bill: only at open tables, a location check, a cap per round and a pace limit per table

Martin 10-19 and 10-20, grilling "Guarding against fake orders on the bill" (#38 of map #29) with the #38 session (Opus) and three research subagents. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-10-19-fake-orders-decision-log.md`. Sources, filed under D114: how other products guard against fake orders (`2026-10-19-fake-order-guards-elsewhere.md`), what a phone's browser gives for location indoors (`2026-10-19-location-check-indoors.md`) and the location check under the ePrivacy rules (`2026-10-19-location-check-eprivacy.md`). Built on D111, D112, D113, D115, D116 and D117.

**The risk.** On-the-bill orders go into the POS unpaid (D113, D115), so anyone holding a table code can put drinks on that table's bill. A photo of the code is enough (tracker B2). This supersedes D27, which relied on every order being paid first. In the one well-documented case, in Kunming in 2023, a table's code was shared online and about 430,000 yuan of orders reached that one table.

**What is guarded, and against whom.** The guards apply only to **on-the-bill** orders. Pay-first orders and paying are never blocked, located or limited, because money coming in is never a risk (D111).
- **An outsider with a photo or link** of a table code is stopped by the location check.
- **Someone inside the venue using another table's code** is narrowed by the cap per round and the pace limit. Location can't catch a person who is inside.
- **A script flooding the order address** is security engineering, not a product guard (Martin: "penetration prevention"): the stack doc's Cloudflare proxy, rate limits and Turnstile. Cloudflare's free rate limit counts per IP address, and a club's guests share one, so that limit stays generous and the real limits are Restro's own.
- **Friends at the same table** ordering on the shared bill is not a fake order: everyone with the table code sees and adds to the bill (D111).

The guards add to one another; none replaces another. Where a venue switches on approval at the bar (D116), the bar also sees each round first. A guard that trips makes the order Not sent, with the reason, and the lines stay in the table's bag (D116, D117).

**Only at open tables.** An on-the-bill order is accepted only at an **open table**: a table with at least one open POS bill, which a waiter has started in the POS.
- On a table with no open POS bill, the table code still shows the menu and pay-first items still order. On-the-bill items say "Ask your waiter to open your table".
- This shuts the easiest hole, which is any empty table at any time, even after the group has gone home. It costs staff no new tap. GonnaOrder and the pay-at-table products work the same way.
- The price is that a table's first on-the-bill round always goes through a waiter.
- It narrows D115's "only when the table has no open POS bill does the order open a new one" to pay-first orders, and to venues that switch this rule off.

**The location check.** Before an on-the-bill order, the phone confirms it is at the venue (D115: Martin's direction).
- **When.** The check runs at the first Send of an on-the-bill order, not when the menu opens. Restro's own line comes first, and it is the consent: "[Venue] and Restro check you're in the venue so nobody can order onto your table from outside. Your location is used once and not stored.", with the button **"Allow and continue"**. The phone's own prompt follows. It names neither who asks nor why, and once a guest refuses it the page can never ask again, so the reason comes first. Every later on-the-bill order checks again without a prompt, so a guest who left can't keep ordering onto the table from home.
- **Passing.** The phone reports a spot and a circle of doubt. The check passes when that circle touches the venue's circle, so it fails only guests who are clearly elsewhere.
  - In a basement there is no GPS. Positions come from Wi-Fi and phone masts and are off by tens to hundreds of metres.
  - A circle wider than about 1 km counts as "can't check". With approximate location (iPhone's Precise Location off, or Android's "approximate"), the phone gives a 3–5 km area, which proves nothing.
- **Not passing.** If the guest refuses, the phone blocks it, the result is "can't check", or no answer comes in about 10 seconds, that phone sends no on-the-bill order. The check fails closed. The screen offers **Call a waiter** and **Cancel**, which goes back to the bag:
  - Call a waiter takes visually more space, though not huge, and Cancel never looks hidden.
  - A small "Why?" line says the venue couldn't be confirmed and how to allow location next time.
  - The menu, the bill and paying are never blocked, and pay-first items still pay and send as normal.
  - If the field test shows that real guests often can't be located inside, this one point is revisited.
- **Where it's checked.** The phone sends its position with the order. The server checks it in memory and drops it: it is never written anywhere, logs included (D115, D111). Checking on the server means a casual tinkerer must fake coordinates rather than flip one "yes", and the rule stays in one place.
- **What it can't do.** A location is easy to fake for anyone who knows how, and no server can tell. The check stops the prankster at home with a photo, not someone on the street outside or a determined faker. No product researched does this check; it is Restro's own.
- **First, a field test in the pilot club** (Martin runs it). Readings come from an iPhone and an Android phone, with Wi-Fi on and off and precise location on and off. They are taken at spots inside, including the deepest corner, and on the street 50, 100 and 200 m away. The radius and the "can't check" width are set from the numbers. Until then the radius is 150 m: Android's own guidance puts 100 m as the smallest useful circle.
- **The law.** The location read falls under the ePrivacy rules (Art. 5(3) of the Directive, in Bulgaria Art. 4а of the Electronic Commerce Act, ЗЕТ). The "strictly necessary" exemption probably does not fit a check that protects the venue and other guests, so the build follows the consent design above. The privacy notice (T31) covers the check.
  - Six questions of interpretation go to the lawyer (T33, Q83): the exemption, whether this consent is valid, whether a refusal may block on-the-bill orders, Turnstile, who is controller and whether a DPIA is needed, and who enforces Art. 4а ЗЕТ.

**Someone inside using another table's code.**
- **A cap per on-the-bill round** (one Send of the table's bag, D117): €150 by default. Above it the guest pays first or asks the waiter, which is how bottle service usually goes anyway. The venue sets the amount or switches the cap off.
- **A pace limit per table:** at most 6 on-the-bill rounds from phones per table per 15 minutes. Beyond that, the phones see "Ask your waiter" until the pace drops. A real table doesn't order every 2½ minutes for a quarter of an hour.
- **The last line of defence** is the waiter's existing reversal. The table says "not ours" and the waiter takes it off in the POS. Restro's rounds are entered by its own POS user (rule 9), so staff can tell them apart.
- **Rejected:** "one phone, one table per night", because a reservation can span several tables, and clubs already face this without Restro (Martin).
- **Built only if a venue asks:** a PIN on the back of the table card, or codes that expire. Inside a club anyone can turn the card over, the PIN adds a step for every guest, location already covers outsiders, and expiring codes need screens or staff work.

**What staff see.** Staff get no live signal and no new screen when a guard trips (D107); the guest's phone says why. Restro counts every trip per venue and table. A table that hits the pace limit alerts the Restro team, in the way #39 decides for alerts at night.

**What a venue can change (D107).** One box in venue settings, **"Ordering on the bill"**. Martin: all of it is the venue's to set, because it's the venue's floor and the venue's risk. Defaults and limits keep a venue from refusing its own guests by mistake. "More" keeps the box simple. There are no choices per area, for the same simplicity as D113's one switch per table.
- **Shown:**
  - "Only at tables a waiter has opened": on.
  - "Largest round on the bill without paying first": €150, or off.
  - The venue's spot: "I'm at the venue – use this spot" (one tap on the owner's phone, inside) or a pin on a map.
- **Under a small "More":**
  - "Check guests are in the venue": on. Switching it off shows one line: "Anyone with a photo of a table code could order onto that table's bill".
  - "How far from the spot counts as inside": 150 m, then the field test's number, settable from 100 to 500 m.
  - "Most rounds per table in 15 minutes": 6, settable from 3 to 20, or off.
