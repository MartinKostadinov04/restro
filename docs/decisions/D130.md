# D130: The design option set: 48 options on 14 pages, and the never-list

Martin 10-27, through the lead (Fable). Sources: the decision log `docs/deliverables/2026-10-27-menu-designer-decision-log.md` (rows 1.12, 1.17, 1.18, 1.22, 2.1, 2.5–2.7); the options spec `2026-10-27-menu-design-options-spec.md` as amended by the independent review `2026-10-27-menu-design-options-review.md`; the component inventory `2026-10-27-guest-menu-component-inventory.md`.

The rule for an option: (a) a venue owner would plausibly want it to differ between venues, and (b) one renderer can draw every value well on a phone from 320 px up, with every floor of the designer guide (§7.5) kept. "The set that makes sense, not every minor detail" (Martin). Values are short lower-case words, the first one Basic's. The contract file that mirrors this table is the binding list in code; this decision is the binding list in words, and the two are kept equal by a test.

- **Basic's values** are the first value of each row (row 2.6 of the log). **What Editorial, Soft, Classic and Club set** is the spec's §4 table with the review's defaults; the spec's four "Editorial as today" errors (the pay scope, the call kinds, Send and Pay fills, the receipt's rules) are corrected when the Styles are written, so Editorial reproduces today's look.
- **The Photos page is checked against the Platform's image code** (`component_options.image`, `masthead.image_*`) when the spec issue is written, so nothing the passport offers is lost (row 2.7). Done in the spec (#168, 10-28): the five options hold; the backdrop semantics (drawn on the photo's box; tint is the accent at 10 % into the page; white on both screens) and "a bleed photo draws no corners of its own" are renderer rules. The focal point stays per photo, set when it is uploaded in Structure.
- **Ditched, fixed at a default** (the review, accepted as a block): the header's written title (shown); a status-panel look (centred); the bill's frame (flat) and total place (bottom); the pay scope (stacked rows), methods (list) and promo field (folded); the receipt's layout (detailed). Values lost: header band *accent*, depth *high*, call kinds *words*, bill rows *open*.
- **Renderer rules, not options:** the price follows the name size one step down; an ink button on an ink surface inverts; two-column cards draw the choices summary and extra fields; with allergen names the footer lists names; pills on a band use the band's pair; the host's notices and D122's consent and last-orders sheets inherit General; compact bill lines keep the 12 px floor; the rail's alignment follows the header, the marks' place is derived, one stepper per renderer.
- **The never-list, a contract clause tested on every Style at every value.** No option may hide, move, re-colour, resize below its floor or change: an item's name (never cut); its price and "from" (in the ink, never struck through, never smaller than the body text); the allergens and a way to read their meaning; every part an item has, on its row (R8; nothing is taken off the row); the state label of every drawn item and "Ask your waiter"; the order of sections, items, groups and options (R2), every placement drawn once (R7); the closed-menu note, the table label, the language switch with two or more languages, every guest-feature control whose feature is on (R15); the amounts and lines on the bill, the quote and the receipt (D72, D122), the waiter reference at 40 px and 7:1, the consent text, the "Preview" marker; the §7.5 floors (12 px text, 16 px inputs, 44 px targets, 4.5:1 text and 3:1 control pairs, visible focus, reduced motion, `lang`, no horizontal scroll at 320 px, theme tokens only). Sold-out and unavailable items are not drawn at all (D131). Allergens are numbers or names now, icons later, never behind one button only.
- **Stripe's Payment Element** takes General's colours, body font, corners and density through its Appearance API; the Apple Pay and Google Pay buttons keep their own look by Apple's and Google's rules (height, radius and a black/white theme only). Click to edit stops at Stripe's frame.
