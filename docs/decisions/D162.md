# D162: A song request has one price the venue sets, and no amounts are shown to anyone

Martin, 11-22 to 11-24, in the grilling of the Club Biad song-request prototype. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (rows 1.2, 1.3, 1.10, 1.10a). Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520). Row 1.1 of that log sets the frame for D162 to D171: every point of D99 was re-decided in the grilling, and what came out of it "replaces D99 where the two differ".

## Decided

- **One price.** Release 1 ships a **single price for a request**, a venue setting, in integer minor units through the `money` module (rule 8). No free-text bid, no price steps, no minimum-and-above.
- **Free requests are a venue setting** (row 1.2), queued behind every paid one. **Off by default wherever a price is set** (the lead's default, row 1.9): a free request behind every paid one cannot reach the DJ on a busy night, so leaving it on would publish a place number that cannot move. Where a venue turns it on, a free request shows no place number at all.
- **No amounts anywhere on screen** (row 1.3). One price makes them moot, so no guest, and no screen in the room, shows what anybody paid. A guest sees what he himself is paying, and nothing else.
- **The guest's name is optional, and showing it is his own choice** (row 1.3). The venue decides whether names are shown at all (D171); where they are, each guest chooses whether his own shows, request by request. TouchTunes' "Anonymous" is the same idea.
- **Bidding, price tiers, chip-ins and partial capture are not built.** Nothing in release 1 orders a queue by money, shows an amount to a third party, or captures part of a payment. The structure stays open to all of them (below); walking through that door is a later decision, not a licence.

## What must stay true, so tiers or bidding stay reachable (row 1.10a)

The service layer already keeps payments as their own rows with append-only steps beside them, all written through `applyService` (D122 §9), which is why one price, tiers, bidding and chip-ins are the same structure. Five things are cheap now and expensive to retrofit:

1. **Store the amount on every request, always,** in integer minor units — never infer it from the venue's current setting.
2. **Order by (priority, asked-at), never by a stored position.** With one price every request's priority is equal, so the order is arrival order and nothing has to change later.
3. **A pricing policy that is a list with one row today, not a `price` column on the venue.** The most important of the five, and it costs a table instead of a column.
4. **Record which policy priced each request** (rule 2's provenance). A venue that changes its price mid-night must not re-price the requests already waiting.
5. **A request has payments, plural, from the first commit** — one today, no chip-in screen, no succession, no partial capture. This is the one that cannot be retrofitted cheaply, because it turns a request from having a payer into having backers. It is also what makes D166's popularity cheap rather than optional.

## Why

Martin asked for the standard price himself, and then went further than the question: one price, a single setting. It removes the whole ranking machinery — order by total, chip-in, succession, partial capture — from release 1, and with it every screen that would have had to show money. Bidding is also something **no product in the research does** (`docs/deliverables/2026-11-18-song-requests-how-others-do-it.md`), and the market leader deliberately prices in tiers it controls instead.

What stays a redesign rather than a migration: the guest's screen. Bidding would bring amounts back on screen and this decision's second rule with them. That is a screen plan under D123, not an architecture problem.

## Not decided here

Guest accounts (row 1.19, `later`: "once we become a recognisable solution"; D9 forbids them today). Restro's own charge to the venue, which belongs to the platform's charging model. The venue is the seller, as D98 has it.
