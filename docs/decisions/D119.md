**The law, as Restro builds on it.**
- **Consent is needed.** Reading the phone's location falls under ePrivacy Art. 5(3); in Bulgaria that is Art. 4а of the Electronic Commerce Act (ЗЕТ). The exemption for what a requested service "strictly needs" is arguable for an anti-fraud check, but not safe to rely on. This replaces D118's "probably does not fit".
- **Refusing may block on-the-bill orders.** A guest who refuses can still pay first or call a waiter, so the consent stays free.
- **Who controls what.** The venue and Restro jointly control the location check. Restro processes the orders on the venue's behalf.
- **Who enforces.** The consumer protection commission (КЗП) enforces Art. 4а ЗЕТ. A company is fined BGN 500–2,000, or BGN 1,000–4,000 when repeated. The data protection commission (КЗЛД) enforces the GDPR in parallel.
- This corrects the two earlier research files, which named КЗЛД as the enforcer and the Electronic Communications Act as the law.

**The consent screen** replaces D118's line and its "Allow and continue" button. It is short, and the details sit behind the privacy link:
> **[Venue] and Restro check your location each time you order to the bill, so nobody outside can add to it.** It's never stored. You can stop anytime, or pay first or call a waiter instead. [Privacy]
> **[Allow]** [Not now]

The law needs four things on the screen before consent: who checks and why, that the location isn't stored, the alternatives, and that the guest can stop. A longer version was rejected as too long (Martin).
- **Once per phone per service day.** The screen shows even when the browser already allows location from an earlier visit, because the consent is to the venue and Restro, not to the browser.
- **A browser that blocks location counts as a refusal.** After a refusal the phone isn't asked again that service day.
- **"Stop location check"** is a small control in the "Why?" line and on the bill. After it, on-the-bill orders from that phone take D118's refused path: Call a waiter, or Cancel.
- **Proof of consent.** Each on-the-bill order records that the location check passed under consent text version N. The record holds no coordinates and no phone identifier. It sits on Restro's own order record, which is kept 18 months (D111).

**The paperwork before the first guest.** An agent drafts it as part of T31, without a lawyer for now:
- the privacy notice items: each party and its role, the joint arrangement and a contact, consent as the basis, the check at every order with nothing stored, how to stop and the alternatives, Turnstile and Cloudflare's role, and complaints to КЗЛД;
- a short privacy impact assessment (DPIA) by Restro for all venues;
- Restro's records of processing, plus a template entry for venues;
- a venue-terms clause on joint control of the check (GDPR Art. 26) and processing of orders (Art. 28).

**Turnstile.**
- It runs only at Send, never on the menu pages.
- It runs in managed mode, with "remember this browser" (pre-clearance) and feedback off.
- What it stores on the phone is inspected on staging.
- Cloudflare is asked in writing whether its reuse of the signals to improve bot detection can be switched off.
- No home-made alternative for now: it would be a new dependency.
