# D168: The DJ opens and closes the door to new requests, there is no automatic cap, and the same song cannot be asked again at once

Martin, round 2 of the grilling of the Club Biad song-request prototype, extended in round 4. Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md` (rows 1.16, 1.17, 1.21). Spec: [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520), feature sets 6 and 9.

## Decided

- **The DJ opens and closes the door to new requests himself,** from his own screen, during the night. This is the one control that governs intake.
- **The venue setting is only the automatic stop** — how long before the planned end of the night requests close by themselves, 30 minutes by default (D171). The DJ's door is his, over and above it. Re-opening within that guard is refused, as the prototype refuses it.
- **No automatic cap on intake** (row 1.21). On a night that takes 180 requests and plays 40, nothing stops taking them by itself. What keeps that honest is D163's ninety-minute release: nobody waits all night on a request that will not arrive, whatever the DJ does.
- **His screen shows him the arithmetic** (settled by the lead after round 4): how many are waiting against how many he has played tonight, so closing the door is an informed act rather than a guess. A number on a screen, not a rule.
- **The same song cannot be asked again within a venue-set time,** 30 minutes by default (row 1.17, D171). The cooldown starts when the song plays. It applies to a **fresh** ask, never to supporting a request already waiting (D166).
- **Who may open and close requests** is not a new role: the roles that already hold the ordering pause.

## Why

An automatic cap would have to guess how fast the DJ plays, and the number that matters — how many he can get through tonight — is a thing only he knows. The prototype already works this way: opening and closing intake is a DJ action, with a 30-minute guard against re-opening near the planned end, and a per-song cooldown set when a song starts. Both are keeps, with a venue setting over them.

The cooldown is also the cap on D166's popularity: a group can move a song up, but not have it played six times.

## Not decided here

Anything the DJ tunes mid-night beyond the door and the size of his working set (D164).
