# D117: One shared bag per table, guarded by server locks; pay-first lines stay in the bag until someone pays them

> **Refined by D120 (10-20, #39):** the table pause after an Unconfirmed order lifts when a waiter acts on the table in the POS, a staff member taps "Resume table", the table's POS bills close, or 15 minutes pass. A venue pause treats Waiting orders the same way.

Martin 10-19 and 10-20, grilling "The life of an order and what the guest sees" (#37 of map #29). Decision log: `docs/deliverables/2026-10-19-order-life-decision-log.md`, sections 2, 4–6. Sources, filed under D114: Fable on one shared bag per table (`2026-10-19-shared-table-bag.md`), Fable on Martin's pay-first flow (`2026-10-19-pay-first-in-shared-bag.md`), an Opus proposal for the locks (`2026-10-19-table-bag-locking.md`) and Fable's second opinion on it (`2026-10-19-bag-locks-second-opinion.md`). An order's states and what the guest sees are D116.

**One bag per table.** Every phone that opens the guest page from a table code sees and edits the same **bag**, kept on Restro's server (Martin: "the session is one - the bag looks the same for every single phone"). The **bill** is the history of what the table ordered (D115); the bag is what the table is preparing to order. Toast, Wolt, Uber Eats and Mews keep one shared cart the same way. A bag per phone, with a second phone's order waiting its turn, was proposed first and replaced.
- **No "who added it".** A line doesn't remember which phone added it (Martin: "I dont see the added value"). Anyone at the table may edit, remove, send or pay any line. The Send button says "Send 5 items"; removing a line shows "Removed · Undo" for a few seconds. Identical lines merge ("2 × Gin tonic").
- **Send moves the on-the-bill lines out of the bag into one order at once,** so nobody edits an order on its way. Two Sends at once make one order. A Send is refused with "Your table's bag changed" only if a line it sends changed in the second before; the phone refreshes and the guest taps again.
- **One order in flight per table** (D115). A Send while the table's previous order is still going in is not refused: its order is Waiting ("Sending…") and goes in when the first ends. If the first ends Unconfirmed, the table's ordering pauses and the waiting order becomes Not sent ("Ordering is paused, please call a waiter"), its lines back in the bag.
- **The duplicate warning.** A Send whose items and quantities match an order from the same table in the last 15 minutes first asks "Your table ordered the same 4 minutes ago. Send again?"; a Pay matching a payment from the bag likewise. It catches two friends ordering the same round, and a re-order after "couldn't send" that may have landed after all. The 15 minutes is a starting value.

**Locks.** Every action where several people act on one thing (editing, adding, removing, sending, paying) is guarded by a lock held on Restro's server with a deadline, and every phone refreshes once it is released (Martin). No phone ever holds a lock, so a dead phone or a crashed server never leaves a table stuck.
- **Single edits** (add, +1, remove, undo, restore) take the bag for about a hundredth of a second and are put in order; both of two taps at once count. Every phone shows the change within 2–3 seconds. No lock is held while a guest edits: a bag has no "done editing" moment, so such a lock could only end by timeout and would block the other phones (Fable).
- **Send** holds the table's order turn while its order is Sending or Checking (up to about a minute and a half) and shows "Sending…" with no countdown, which would promise a time the POS doesn't keep. An order Awaiting approval (D116) no longer holds it.
- **Paying** is a visible countdown lock, one for a payment from the bag and one for a payment of the bill, so the two can run side by side (D112: one payment at a time per bag and per bill). Every phone shows "Someone at your table is paying · 1:42" with those lines greyed; the paying phone sees the same clock. The guest has 2 minutes to reach the bank's page, then the venue's unfinished-payment time (default 10 minutes, D112); it can't be extended. When time runs out before the bank's page opened, the paying guest is brought back to the payment screen: "Your time ran out. Nothing was charged." with "Pay again". After the bank's page opened, "Checking…" until the provider's notice, with no retry (D112).
- **Only the paying phone may cancel** ("Pay later"). It carries a random token for the payment's lifetime only, in its own storage, never on orders, payments or logs; it never marks lines and is never shown (D111: nothing about a phone outlasts the service day).
- **Countdowns come from the server's "seconds left"** in every update; phones never use their own clock. Questions on screen close by themselves: the duplicate warning closes after 60 seconds and returns the guest to the bag.
- How the bag reaches the phone (one combined call with the bill, polling about every 2 seconds on the bag and bill screens, or push), the guest commands and the refusal codes are the guest API's (#40), starting from the Opus proposal.

**How long a bag lives.** The bag is Restro's own, not the POS's, so Restro says when it ends:
- when it is sent or emptied;
- after an idle time the venue sets, default 30 minutes, restarted by every edit and by Send. A timed-out bag moves aside as **"Earlier bag · Restore"**, seen by every phone at the table, until all the table's POS bills close. The venue sets how many earlier bags are kept, default 1 (the latest);
- when all the table's open POS bills close, usually because the group paid and left (Martin: the only way something visible to the guest is wiped). The bag and its earlier bags go;
- at the end of the service day (07:00), and when the venue makes a new table code for the table.

The cost is small: one row per table reused all night, about 200 KB and 6,000–12,000 small writes a night for a 50-table club (Opus).

**Pay-first lines stay in the bag until someone pays them.** This replaces D112's "the guest chooses between paying only the pay-first items and paying the whole bag; the on-the-bill items wait until the payment settles" and its "Order the rest without it". It is Martin's design; Fable found it more robust, because a declined card or a minute of "Checking…" no longer holds back drinks that need no payment, and no product researched mixes the two in one order.
- **Send sends the on-the-bill lines at once** as a round. Pay-first lines stay in the bag, marked "not ordered until paid"; nothing pay-first reaches the POS before it is paid. Anyone at the table may pay them (D111). They go with the bag when it ends.
- **One payment for the ticked selection,** with every pay-first line ticked by default; the Pay button beside a line opens the payment with that line ticked. Every payment costs the venue a card fee, a fiscal receipt and a closed POS bill, so one payment means one fee for the whole selection.
- **No yes/no question after Send.** The "Added to your bill" screen says "2 items are not ordered until paid" with "Pay 24 лв". A bag holding only pay-first lines shows "Pay X and order".
- **"Paying…"** runs through the hold and the POS write; **"Paid. Your receipt is at the bar"** shows only after the POS's yes. The bar makes the drink from its own POS ticket, never from a guest's screen. A declined card offers Try again, Pay later or Call a waiter; Pay later puts the lines back.
- **Paying doesn't wait for the table's order turn,** which exists only for D115's read-back of rounds; a paid POS bill is new and stamped (D112). A Send and a Pay can run at the same time.
- **At Pay every ticked line is checked again:** a sold-out line can't be ticked; a changed price refuses the payment once with "The menu changed, check your bag".
- **The minimum card payment** is the venue's setting (D112). Below it the payment screen shows "Add 4 лв more to pay by card" with a few pay-first suggestions, like Uber Eats' minimum order, and Pay unlocks at the minimum. Which items are suggested, and how it looks, is the bill screen's (#45).

Unchanged from D112: a pay-first order is its own POS bill, opened, paid and closed in one call; hold, write, capture; paying your own items on the bill is a split.
