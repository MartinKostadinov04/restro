# D135: The night on the plan: arrivals, walk-ins, the waiting list, changing tables, and blocks

Martin and the Port flow lead session, 10-31 to 11-07. Sources as in D132; decision log Features 3, 4 and 5, with rounds 3 to 7. Their MVP asked for a guest count at every arrival, had three different names for replacing a party's tables, and refused to let a party move back to the waiting list.

## Martin's decisions

- **Arrived is one tap** (4.20): the party is seated, all its tables go red, and the guest count is optional and changeable later. More guests than seats warns (D132); a booking for another date warns.
- **Seating the party is what opens the table's menu** (12.12). There is no separate "open the table" tap on the hostess's plan: marking the guests present on their table is what makes that table's code live, and the guest can order.
- **A walk-in** (3.21): the hostess puts the party on the spot, no name needed, and the table goes red as used. It happens now, so there is no date or time to fill in, and the table's code opens as on any arrival.
- **A party can be seated on a table reserved later that night** (3.26): the hostess sees who holds it and from when, and one tap seats them with their end time at that hour. Two parties on one table at the same time are refused, naming who is in the way. The venue's turn time (D133) can turn the early seating off or set the shortest window.
- **A waiter opening a table nobody was seated at is warned**, and afterwards the table goes red on the hostess's plan so she seats nobody else; if the table is reserved, his warning names the reservation and its hour (3.22, 3.27).
- **The waiting list is a simple queue** (3.23, 3.24): a party goes on the list for the night asked about, and if they are open to another night the hostess adds them there separately — nothing is automated. A free table offers the waiting parties in the order added, and the hostess picks and calls by hand. Automation is reviewed when the process is known in detail.
- **Taking a table back from a party, or a waiting party leaving, uses the standard operations** (3.25): no special workflow and no extra status.
- **One "Change tables" screen** replaces Move, Add places and Choose places (4.21): the hostess taps tables on and off, the reservation moves, and the new table's code opens. This is the reservation stage only.
- **Moving a party that has already ordered is a bill move, not a reservation move** (4.22): staff pick the bill up in the POS and move it to the other table, through Tonight as a waiter would (D110, D122). Restro opens and closes the digital menus to match — the old table's code closes, the new one's opens.
- **Marking a spot free closes its table codes**, with a warning if the bill is still open (1.48), and freeing waits for an unsettled payment (1.65).
- **A block is a reservation on that table** (5.1, 5.20), with no special workflow: optional from and until dates, staying on until removed when none are set, and a Manager permission a venue can give a hostess. It cannot be placed where a party is already booked — the hostess moves that party with the normal Change tables — and a party sitting there now keeps the table until it leaves. A blocked table leaves online booking and warns on Tonight.

## The lead's calls

- **What closes a table's menu** (12.14), unchanged from how ordering already works, because ending a table's sitting throws away the guests' cart, their sent order and their phones' location passes:
  - the everyday closer stays the bill disappearing from the POS on a complete reading (D122 §4);
  - freeing the spot closes the codes, with the open-bill warning;
  - a reservation move closes the old table and opens the new one.
  - Nothing else on the plan closes a table. There is no "open on seating" setting: seating always opens (13.2), since with it switched off a hostess would mark guests present and have no way to open a menu at all.
- **The explicit open and close stay on the Tonight screen** (12.15), for what seating cannot cover: a table a waiter opens with nobody seated, a spot on no plan, and a role that may open tables without touching reservations.
- **Seating a party onto a table that is already open** (12.13, 13.6) asks whether these are the same guests: yes keeps the sitting and its cart, no sends "New guests". Without the question the new party would inherit the previous group's cart, its sent order and its phones' location passes — and sending "New guests" blindly would throw away a round the waiter had already rung.
- **More people joining a party already seated is not "New guests"** (12.21): nothing on the table changes, the sitting stands, their cart survives, and only the reservation's guest count rises.
- **The open-bill warning fires at arrival and on Change tables too** (12.22, 13.8), not only when a table is freed: the check before opening a table looks at payments, hidden and vanished tables, never at a bill left open, so the new party's first order would otherwise land on the old bill.
- **Marking a party present on a table other than the one it booked is one gesture on the plan** (13.7), not Change tables followed by Arrived: it is the commonest thing that happens at the door.
- **The warning on a waiter-opened table fires only when that table is reserved later tonight or blocked** (13.1): a warning on every open is clicked through by the second hour.
- **Swapping two parties stays one step, inside the same Change tables screen** (4.23): tapping a table that belongs to another party asks to swap with it. A swap done by hand needs a free table in between, which a full club does not have.
- **Copy to another night stays** (4.24), as a small action on the reservation: it keeps the details, leaves out arrivals and prepayment, and lets the hostess pick other tables when those are taken. A walk-in cannot be copied, having no details.
- **A role without the open-and-close permission still marks Arrived** (1.65), with one line saying the table stayed closed; Tonight shows both states.
- **A party on several tables is, as ordering stands today, several open tables with a bill each** (12.18). Reservations must not assume otherwise. The one cumulative total is D137's work, filed as its own ordering ticket.
