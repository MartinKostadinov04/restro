# D135: A reservation and its life: its statuses, who may work it, and the pay rule it carries

Martin and the Port flow lead session, 10-31 to 11-07. Sources as in D134; decision log Feature 2, with rounds 5 to 7. Their MVP had eight statuses, no cancel button, no way back from a no-show, and accepted bookings on any date in the past.

## Martin's decisions

- **The statuses are Requested, Reserved, Waiting, Seated, Finished, No-show and Cancelled** (log 2.22). Requested is an online booking nobody has answered yet. **Cancelled means the guest cancelled** — it is not how staff tidy up.
- **A name is required; a phone is required by default**, with a switch in reservation settings to make it optional (2.26).
- **Deleting is a real button, and hard to press by mistake** (2.23, 12.4). A confirmation names the party; the reservation leaves every list; the Log keeps who deleted it and when. Only a manager brings one back. Deleting works on the PIN, with the Log naming the person.
- **Who may work a booking** (2.24, 13.18): anyone with the reservations permission works tonight's and future bookings, **whoever made them** — the hostess on duty cancels, edits and seats a booking her colleague took, and a booking the website confirmed by itself has no maker to belong to. **Deleting someone else's booking, and restoring one, stay the manager's.** Martin's hierarchy stays where it was aimed: the destructive acts.
- **Nobody books in the past** (2.25, 4.25, 13.19). A night that has passed is read-only: no booking, no edit, no deletion. It is read in the Log and in the month of plans (D141). Future nights have no limit, and the screen opens on today's date each time, to lower the chance of working on the wrong night.
  - A venue that does want corrections switches on **editing the past** in its settings: one switch over every past-editing ability, manager-class (13.19).
- **A reservation holds its spots to the end of the night by default, with a custom end time** that lets the spot be booked again after it. When that hour passes, staff are warned and the spot is never freed by itself (2.20, 1.44).
- **Each table has its own schedule** inside the venue's working time, and any part of it can be reserved — clubs book by the night, but timeframes stay possible (2.33).
- **A late party is warned** on the list and on the plan, and its spots are never freed automatically (2.28).
- **A new booking whose details match another that night warns, and never refuses** (2.29): one contact can organise separate groups, and we cannot do much more than say so.
- **The pay rule on a reservation** (1.39, 1.61, 1.62): someone with the permission sets the venue's rule (the default), everything on the bill, or pay-first. It is a fourth level on D122 §5, lasts for that sitting, and every change goes in the Log; a guest can never change it. It needs a Manager-level permission a venue can give a hostess, a table's own rule still wins and the screen says so, and caps and pace still apply. It reaches a new sitting only after staff confirm it is still the same party ("Is this still Maria's party?").
- **Switching over from what the club uses today** (11.1, 11.20): existing confirmed bookings are typed in by hand. No import, no connector, no period of running both. "We cannot automate everything; once they use only us that won't be an issue."

## The lead's calls

- **How long a table is held and the shortest gap before a later booking are one setting**, the table's turn time, as the trade keeps it (13.4; it had been written twice, as 2.20 and 3.26).
- **The night list is sorted by arrival time**, not by when a booking was made as their MVP did; the calendar's shape goes to the screen plan (2.21).
- **Cancel and Delete are told apart in one line each, on the buttons** (13.12): Cancel is the guest's doing and keeps the booking on the night; Delete removes a booking that should never have existed.
- **Any action may carry a reason; only blocking requires one** (4.6), as their MVP had it.
