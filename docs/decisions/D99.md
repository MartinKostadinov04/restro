# D99: Song requests, the first direction (10-17): the paid bidding queue below is superseded by D162 to D171

> **Do not build anything from the model below.** Martin re-decided every point of this decision in the
> grilling of 11-22 to 11-24 — `docs/deliverables/2026-11-22-song-requests-decision-log.md`, row 1.1:
> D99's direction "binds nothing in this grilling", and what came out of it "replaces D99 where the two
> differ". The decisions that bind are **D162 to D171**, and the spec built on them is
> [#520](https://github.com/MartinKostadinov04/restro/issues/520). What follows is kept only as the record
> of where the feature started.
>
> **Dead. None of this exists and none of it is to be written:**
>
> | What D99 said | What binds instead |
> | --- | --- |
> | The queue is ordered by total amount; a guest bids what he likes above a minimum | **D162**: one price, a single venue setting. No bidding, no tiers, no minimum-and-above. |
> | Every guest sees each request's total amount and its backers' names | **D162**: no amounts anywhere on screen; a guest's name is shown only if he chooses. |
> | A guest boosts a request already in the queue, raising its total | **D166**: he pays the same one price and the request moves up by the **number of distinct guests**, never by money. |
> | A lock window of the playing request plus the next N, which the DJ locks and accepts by explicit action | **D164**: the top N fill the awaiting-play set by themselves, with **no order inside it** and a free choice for the DJ. There is no accept gate. |
> | The money is captured when the DJ accepts the request at the top of the window; a skip after accept is refunded | **D163**: captured only when the DJ marks the song **played**; otherwise **released** — on refusal, after ninety minutes, or by the night's sweep. A release is never a refund. |
> | Holds are released only when the queue closes | **D163**: ninety minutes releases a hold by itself, whatever the DJ does. |
> | Venue settings are requests on or off and the minimum paid request, with no further caps | **D171**: ten settings with their defaults; **D168**: the DJ's door, the automatic stop and the same-song cooldown. |
> | The entry point for a guest without a table is undecided (T81) | **D170**: the venue's menu carries the link, and no table is written onto a request. |
>
> **Still standing from D99, not re-decided and not replaced:**
>
> - **Guests need no account, no e-mail address and no phone number** (D9). Identity is D121's guest session (D165).
> - **The DJ is an organisation role** with access only to the request queue of its venues; **D100 refines that access**, and D157 governs what a role opens.
> - **Payment runs through the venue's own payment connection** — Apple Pay, Google Pay, card — and Restro stores no card (D163, D125, D126, D127). The venue is the seller (D98).
> - **Songs are found through the prototype's music-only search** (songs only), with the text and the link shown to the DJ (D167).
> - **One queue per venue per service day**, opened by its first request and closed at the service day's end or earlier by the DJ (D164, D168).
> - **Integration with DJ software or any player is out of scope.**
> - **A song request is a guest feature** (D71) and follows D72; it is a module switched on per venue (D155).
> - **Each change to a venue's queue is one command in one transaction**, captures and releases run as outbox jobs with idempotency keys, and the queue the phones read is a published file polled every few seconds, not a realtime channel. The write path is `applyService` (D122 §9).

> **Superseded, below:**

Guests ask the DJ for songs from their phone (Martin 10-17). A venue's **request queue** holds the service day's song requests in play order: paid requests by total amount, free requests behind every paid one in arrival order. A **lock window** (the playing request plus the next N, N a venue setting, default 1) cannot be displaced by any request or boost; the DJ locks, accepts, skips and pins by explicit action, never by timer.

**Money.** A hold is placed on the card when a paid request or a boost is made; the money is captured when the DJ accepts the request at the top of the window; a request skipped after accept is refunded; holds on requests never reached are released when the queue closes. One song request can have several **backers**: a guest who picks a song already in the queue is told so and may boost it under their own name, pick another song, or cancel. Joining and boosting stop once the request enters the lock window. Each change to a venue's queue is one command applied in one transaction with a lock on that venue's queue; captures and refunds run as outbox jobs with idempotency keys; the queue the phones and the DJ see is a projection published as a live overlay file and polled every few seconds (no realtime channel).

**Guests.** No accounts, no email, no phone. Payment through the provider's checkout (Apple Pay, Google Pay, card); Restro keeps the provider's token and an optional display name. Songs are found through the YouTube search already built in the prototype (songs only), with the text and link shown to the DJ. Every guest sees the whole queue: position, song, total amount, backers' display names; their own requests carry a status (held, in the window, played, skipped, refunded). Venue settings: requests on or off, and the minimum paid request; no further caps.

**The night.** The queue opens with the first request of a service day and closes at the service day's end, or earlier by the DJ. At close every unplayed hold is released and the owner sees the night's totals; the money is at the provider.

**The DJ** is an organisation role (D100 refines access) who opens a plain web page: the queue, now playing and next, accept, skip, pin, grouped duplicates, the night's totals, close night. Integration with DJ software or any player is out of scope.

**Not decided here:** how a guest without a table reaches the request page (a link, a QR on the club's screens, a share button; T81), and the charging model (Q77). The prototype that exists outside this repository is ported into the platform under this decision (T82); song requests are a guest feature (D71) and follow D72.
