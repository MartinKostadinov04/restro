# D134: Booking online, and money on a reservation: the booking page, the prepayment, and the receipt behind it

Martin and the Port flow lead session, 10-31 to 11-07. Sources as in D132; decision log Feature 2 (2.27, 2.30 to 2.36), Feature 7, and rounds 5 to 7. Their MVP had no online booking at all, and a deposit that was information only. The verdict's "staff tool first" is overturned: Martin is having the club's own website built, so guests book online from the start.

## Martin's decisions

- **Guests book online** (2.30). The club's website links to or embeds a booking page Restro hosts, so bookings land directly in Restro with nothing to sync. This amends D38's "QR to phone only": the booking page is a second guest surface, reached from a link rather than a code. D101's hosted page stands.
- **What guests may do there is a venue setting** (12.7, 2.32): ask for a night only, choose a zone, or pick the table on the plan, which is the full ability. Clubs differ, and the setting is what fits them all.
- **An online booking arrives as Requested and a hostess answers it** (2.31). A venue setting lets a club confirm automatically — but **automatic confirmation applies only where the guest picked his own table**, so the table itself stops the next booking; a booking that arrives without a table always waits for a hostess to give it one (12.7).
- **Everything that happens to a guest's reservation or his money reaches him by email** (12.5, 2.35): confirmed, declined, cancelled, the prepayment taken, a hold released, and the night's end declining a request he never heard about. The page asks for his email. SMS stays a paid option for later (D101).
- **The page is drawn in the venue's Style** by the same renderer as the menu, so a club's booking page matches its menu. **Its branding, layout and flow are fixed across clubs**, with no per-club rework (12.8). The Design lead is warned before it lands.
- **Guests book only inside the venue's working hours** (2.36, 12.19); a hostess may book outside them with a warning. The hours are the venue's existing service hours, not a second copy: reservation settings add only the one-off closed dates.
- **The page shows the club's own prepayment and refund wording**, a venue setting, so the guest reads it before he pays (7.22).

### The prepayment

- **A prepayment switch** in reservation settings, changeable on each reservation, with an amount (2.27). It is paid through Stripe on the venue's own account (D98: the venue is the merchant and Restro never holds the money).
- **The money is held, not taken, until the booking is answered.** Where the venue's settings make confirmation mandatory, Confirm takes it and Decline releases it; where the venue confirms automatically, it is taken at booking. The booking reads "Prepayment pending" and warns; it never cancels itself (2.27).
- **The warning goes 48 hours before that payment's own expiry** (12.3), not after a fixed five days: the provider gives each payment its expiry, and a card hold can be as short as four days and eighteen hours. If a hold lapses, the request stays open, marked as no longer paid, and a later Confirm offers the payment link again (13.10).
- **Taking money means a receipt.** Stripe's email proves a card was charged; it is **not** a Bulgarian fiscal receipt, which is why D125 turns the provider's guest receipts off and D127 emails the POS's receipt instead (12.2).
- **A booking taken by phone** gets a payment link by email from the platform, and reads "Prepayment pending" until he pays; the hostess may instead mark it paid in cash, which goes in the Log (2.34).
- **One money record on the reservation** (7.20): an amount, how it came (cash, card, bank transfer, online) and whether it has been given back. Filled in by hand, or marked by itself when an online payment succeeds. Their five states and hidden method serve nobody.
- **Deleting or cancelling a reservation that has money on it asks what happens to the money and logs the answer**; a no-show and the night's end never touch it (7.21).
- **Refunds stay the club's own policy**, with Restro giving it the means and no more (6.23): the venue refunds in its Stripe dashboard, Stripe's notice reaches Restro, and the refund is recorded against the reservation with its storno to-do. Restro's key cannot refund, by D122 §5, D125 and D127.

## The lead's calls

- **A prepayment is a third kind of payment** in the platform's own payment records, with no table, no sitting and no POS step, carrying its reservation instead (12.1). Every payment today is a bill payment at a table, and a prepayment kept anywhere else would make a refund notice match nothing. The provider's notices cover it like any other payment.
- **Confirming a prepayment raises a to-do at the till** to issue the fiscal receipt, with its number typed back onto the reservation (13.21). It is the cautious default: money taken always has a receipt behind it.
- **The booking page keeps rule 4's spirit** (the earlier check's section B): everything still — the venue's name, its Style, its wording, the plan's drawing — comes from the published files, and only what is free or taken comes from **one narrow availability endpoint**. The page never reads the database directly.
  - A booking is written behind Turnstile, as the guest pay route already is (D127 §5.1).
  - A guest's own link to his booking carries a booking token, never an id in an address.
  - Embedding is allowed by naming the venue's own site as the page's permitted frame ancestor; anything else is refused.
- **The reservations list filters on "Prepayment pending"** (13.10), so a hostess can see in one tap who has not paid.

## Open

- **The venue's accountant:** is the till to-do enough for a prepayment's fiscal receipt, and who issues it when the guest never arrives? Until this is answered, prepayment is not switched on at a live venue.
- **The lawyer:** the wording of the refund and prepayment notice the guest accepts.
