# GoTab — Operational & Financial Claims on QR/Mobile Self-Order and Pay
(Bars & Nightclubs Vertical Research — Vendor Marketing Content)

## Summary — Strongest 3-5 Takeaways

GoTab's public marketing repeats three platform-wide headline numbers on almost every page — **$1B+ processed annually, 25% faster table turns, and 35%+ higher check size** — but these are never broken down by venue type or tied methodologically to QR/mobile ordering specifically; treat them as an unsourced aggregate vendor claim, not evidence isolating QR ordering's effect. Where GoTab does cite real venues, the most credible numbers involve **staffing leverage during high volume**: Garfinkel's (Vail, CO) served 1,200 covers on July 4th with two fewer floor staff than normal; Little Pub Company (Denver) serves up to 1,000 guests with just eight bartenders under a hybrid QR/handheld model; The Phoenix Taproom scales from 2-3 staff on a normal day to 5 staff handling 500 guests at peak. A second strong theme is **walkout/unpaid-tab prevention via pre-authorized card-on-file plus fund-locking ("Insufficient Funds Protection")** combined with staff alerts on stale tabs — described consistently as a mechanism across many pages, though never with a quantified reduction in walkout rate. Two named case studies give concrete before/after revenue or labor figures: Aloma Cinema & Grill (21% sales increase, 15% labor-hour reduction) and Green Gables Bar & Grill ($30,000 sales increase in the first month of accepting card payments, i.e. not QR-specific). Genuine peak-throughput numbers appear at Atomic Golf Las Vegas (2,000-3,000 drinks/hour via automated dispensing) and Santa Anita Park (scaling from 5,000-7,000 daily guests to ~110,000 for the Breeders' Cup on the same system). Overall, GoTab's content is heavy on mechanism description (QR ordering, shared/split tabs, RFID self-pour, handheld POS) and light on independently verified, QR-specific quantification — most named case studies avoid stating hard numbers at all.
