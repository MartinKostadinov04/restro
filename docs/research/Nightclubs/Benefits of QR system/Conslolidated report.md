# QR Ordering & Mobile Pay in Bars/Nightclubs — Cross-Vendor Synthesis
### Synthesized from public marketing content of GoTab, me&u, and Toast
*Internal research deliverable — for Restro (Bulgarian nightclub QR ordering) business case development*

---

## How to read this document

Every insight below carries a **confidence tag**, unchanged from the source research:

- **Specific-case-study** — a named venue with a concrete, attributable before/after number or metric.
- **Specific-benchmark** — a quantified figure presented as a general benchmark rather than tied to one venue.
- **Vendor-claim-no-number** — a mechanism or benefit the vendor asserts, with no figure attached.
- **Anecdotal** — a single testimonial, quote, or unquantified impression (often one executive or one operator).

None of these tags should be treated as independent third-party verification — all of it is vendor-published marketing content, including the "Specific-case-study" tier, which typically relies on self-reported venue figures. Nothing here has been upgraded in confidence; where a vendor gave no number, this document says so explicitly rather than implying one.

## 1. Labor cost reduction / staff-hours saved

This is the theme with the most concrete-sounding numbers, but they cluster around **staffing ratios at peak volume** rather than clean "% labor cost reduction" figures — and only one vendor (me&u) ties a number to labor cost directly.

- **GoTab — Aloma Cinema & Grill**: 15% reduction in labor hours alongside a 21% sales increase after adopting GoTab (mechanism not isolated to QR ordering specifically; secondhand reference, original case-study page not directly fetched). — *Specific-case-study* — https://gotab.com/latest/how-to-launch-a-dog-park-bar-or-any-eatertainment-venue-that-actually-works
- **GoTab — Little Pub Company (Denver)**: hybrid table-service + QR + walk-up model lets the venue serve up to 1,000 guests with just eight bartenders, eliminating traditional server-role fragmentation. — *Specific-case-study* — https://gotab.com/latest/raising-a-glass-to-community-little-pub-company-keeps-denvers-neighborhood-bars-thriving-with-gotab
- **GoTab — Garfinkel's (Vail, CO)**: served 1,200 covers on July 4th with two fewer floor staff than normal, running peak service with just three staff on the floor; volume ramped from 25 guests at 12:30pm to 750+ by 1pm. — *Specific-case-study* — https://gotab.com/latest/how-garfinkels-in-vail-co-is-turning-30-years-of-hospitality-into-a-high-tech-advantage
- **GoTab — The Phoenix Taproom**: runs on 2-3 staff on a typical weekday, scales to just 5 staff to handle up to 500 guests at peak without a drop in service. — *Specific-case-study* — https://gotab.com/latest/built-for-volume-swings
- **GoTab — Detroit Shipping Company / The Golden Mill (food hall)**: consolidated multi-vendor late-night service onto one shared station instead of staffing "one person per stall," cutting labor to roughly the cost of one stall instead of seven, while processing 3,000-4,000 tabs nightly with per-vendor automated payouts on one guest check. — *Specific-case-study* — https://gotab.com/latest/how-detroit-shipping-company-solved-late-night-without-adding-staff
- **GoTab — "My Place" (modern hospitality case study)**: over 90% of orders entered tableside via handheld rather than a fixed terminal; operator states he doesn't need to worry much about labor on the venue's self-pour system (qualitative). — *Specific-case-study* / *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://gotab.com/latest/modern-hospitality-business-open-flexible-pos-platform-case-study
- **GoTab — platform-wide claim**: 25% faster table turns, repeated across nearly every marketing page, never disaggregated by venue type or tied methodologically to QR ordering specifically. — *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://gotab.com/latest/revolutionizing-nightlife-gotabs-pos-solutions-for-bars-and-nightclubs (repeated site-wide)
- **me&u — Stoney's Bar & Grill**: Sales Per Labor Hour (SPLH) — a metric that directly nets out labor cost against revenue — rose year-over-year at the Uptown location by 6.5% (Feb), 22.5% (Mar), 7.19% (Apr), and at the OG location by 1.1% (Feb), 2.35% (Mar), 10.23% (Apr). This is the single most rigorous, labor-cost-adjacent metric across all three vendors' public content. — *Specific-case-study* — https://www.meandu.com/us/blog/how-stoneys-bar-grill-uses-me-u-to-combat-labor-costs-and-increase-sales
- **me&u — Stoney's Bar & Grill**: table-side ordering reduces the need for constant staff attention, helping staff keep pace during unexpectedly busy periods without compromising service quality. — *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://www.meandu.com/us/blog/how-stoneys-bar-grill-uses-me-u-to-combat-labor-costs-and-increase-sales
- **Toast**: no labor-cost-as-%-of-revenue figures or staffing ratios were disclosed on any of the 22 resource-center pages reviewed — all such content sits behind gated downloads. Several checklist/SOP pages assert that task standardization enables "more efficient shifts" (Bar Manager Duties checklist) but attach no time or labor figure. — *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://pos.toasttab.com/resources/bar-manager-duties-checklist

**Gap**: none of the three vendors publish a clean "labor cost as % of revenue, before vs. after" figure. GoTab's numbers are staffing ratios at named peak events (not steady-state averages); me&u's SPLH numbers are the most defensible but come from one venue with two locations; Toast discloses nothing quantitative at all in this public tier.

## 4. Walkout & payment-fraud prevention

This theme is documented almost exclusively by GoTab; me&u and Toast do not address it in the material reviewed.

- **GoTab — pre-authorization mechanism**: every tab starts with a pre-authorized card on file (not charged immediately); "Insufficient Funds Protection" pre-authorizes and locks funds against each new order added to a tab. — *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://gotab.com/latest/best-way-to-prevent-walkouts-and-unpaid-bar-tabs
- **GoTab — stale-tab alerting**: staff receive automated alerts if a tab sits inactive for 45 minutes, prompting a check-in or closeout before a guest can leave without paying. — *Vendor-claim-no-number* — https://gotab.com/latest/best-way-to-prevent-walkouts-and-unpaid-bar-tabs
- No quantified walkout-rate reduction, unpaid-tab dollar recovery, or chargeback-rate figure is given anywhere in GoTab's content, and this mechanism is entirely absent from me&u's and Toast's public material.

## Cross-vendor patterns

**Where they converge:**
- All three vendors that discuss ordering technology at all (GoTab, me&u) converge on the same core mechanism claims: QR/table ordering reduces bar-queue congestion, speeds order entry, and creates upsell opportunity through easier repeat-ordering — but neither vendor attaches a rigorous, methodologically transparent number to the QR channel in isolation. Every quantified figure from both vendors is either (a) a platform-wide aggregate with no stated methodology (GoTab's 25%/35% claims), or (b) a single named venue's self-reported before/after, with the underlying comparison period, sample size, and confounds undisclosed.
- Both GoTab and me&u lean heavily on a small number of named case studies to carry their entire quantitative story. GoTab has more of them (roughly a dozen with some real numbers); me&u effectively has one (Stoney's Bar & Grill) carrying almost all its hard figures across two locations and several months.
- Both vendors' industry/landing pages — the place most likely to host an aggregate, defensible stat — conspicuously do not. me&u's own "Pubs & Bars" page states no check-size or order-time figures; GoTab's platform-wide numbers are repeated as taglines without a link to methodology.

**Where they diverge or leave gaps:**
- **Substantiation level differs sharply by vendor.** GoTab publishes the largest number of named venues with concrete, if narrow, metrics (staffing counts, guest counts, dollar figures). me&u publishes far fewer numbers overall but its one case study (Stoney's SPLH data) is arguably the *cleanest* labor-cost-adjacent metric in the entire dataset because it's a ratio (sales per labor hour) rather than a raw headcount or single-month spike. Toast's public resource-center tier contains **zero** quantitative claims of any kind — every number Toast might have is gated behind lead-generation downloads not captured in this research.
- **A hard "labor cost as % of revenue" figure is essentially absent across all three vendors.** This is arguably the single most important metric for a nightclub operator's P&L, and none of the three vendors publish it in a form a reader could independently sanity-check. This is a real gap in vendor evidence, not just a research-scope limitation — it suggests the industry hasn't (publicly) proven this number, or it varies too much to be a usable marketing claim.
- **Walkout/payment-fraud prevention and peak-surge capacity are almost exclusively a GoTab theme.** Neither me&u nor Toast address unpaid-tab or fraud prevention at all in the material reviewed, and only GoTab documents extreme-multiple surge events (Santa Anita's ~15-20x demand swing). This may reflect GoTab's positioning toward higher-volume/eatertainment venues, or simply a gap in what me&u and Toast chose to publish — either way, a Bulgarian nightclub operator evaluating "can this survive a 2,000-person Friday night" will find more raw material from GoTab than from the other two.
- **Nightclub-specific evidence is thin everywhere.** None of the three vendors' case studies are themselves nightclubs in the strict late-night, high-volume, cash-heavy sense — they're breweries, taprooms, neighborhood pubs, food halls, a dog-park bar, a golf-sim venue, and a horse-racing track. Toast's nightclub-specific pages (operations manual, business plan template, cost/margin calculators) are pure lead-gen wrappers with no content. This means every vendor's evidence needs to be treated as adjacent-venue-type evidence, not nightclub-validated evidence.

## Implications for a nightclub-focused QR ordering product in Bulgaria

1. **Do not put GoTab's 25%/35% platform-wide numbers, or any single-venue percentage, into a pitch deck as if they were Restro's expected results.** They are unsourced (GoTab) or single-case (me&u's Stoney's SPLH gains, GoTab's Aloma 21%/15%) figures from non-nightclub US/Australian venues; a Bulgarian nightclub audience (investors, prospective venue partners) will reasonably ask for local validation, and the vendor data does not license a specific expected lift.
2. **The clearest, most defensible metric type across all three vendors is a staffing ratio at peak volume** (e.g., "N staff serving M guests"), not a percentage. If Restro runs a pilot, structure the pilot's core KPI the same way — floor/bar staff count vs. guests served per hour at peak — since this is the one measurement type with multiple independent-ish precedents (GoTab: Garfinkel's, Phoenix Taproom, Little Pub) that a reader can sanity-check without trusting a percentage's hidden baseline.
3. **Sales-per-labor-hour (SPLH) is the single most rigorous ROI metric found in any vendor's public content (me&u/Stoney's)** and is directly computable from data any Bulgarian venue already has (POS sales + scheduled labor hours). This is the strongest candidate KPI for Restro's own pilot measurement — it nets out both revenue and labor in one ratio and is resistant to the "we just got busier" confound that undermines raw sales-increase claims.
4. **A hard labor-cost-as-%-of-revenue figure is a genuine market gap, not just a vendor-marketing omission** — none of GoTab, me&u, or Toast publish one. If Restro can generate this number from even a small pilot (e.g., "labor cost fell from X% to Y% of bar revenue on QR-ordering nights vs. control nights"), it would be a differentiated, defensible claim no competitor is currently making publicly — worth prioritizing as a pilot deliverable specifically because the competitive field has left it open.
5. **Walkout/payment-fraud prevention (pre-authorization + fund-locking + stale-tab alerts) is a mechanism only GoTab documents, and even GoTab gives no quantified walkout-rate reduction.** For a Bulgarian nightclub context — where cash-heavy, high-turnover, late-night operations plausibly make walkouts and disputed tabs a real cost — this is a claim area where Restro could differentiate by actually measuring and publishing a walkout-rate reduction number, since no vendor in this research has done so.
6. **Peak-surge handling is the strongest "can this survive our busiest night" evidence available, but it's evidence of overall system capacity, not proof that QR ordering specifically drove it.** Santa Anita's 15-20x swing and Garfinkel's 30x ramp in 30 minutes are compelling operationally, but in every case the vendor bundles QR ordering together with handheld POS, automated dispensing, and other tech — none isolate QR ordering's marginal contribution. Any pitch-deck claim about peak-night resilience should describe the *system* (QR + payment automation + staff alerting), not attribute the outcome to QR ordering alone.
7. **None of the three vendors' case studies are nightclubs in the strict sense** (late-night, high-volume, cash- and card-mixed, minimal food component, dense crowd). Treat every vendor number in this report as adjacent-venue-type evidence at best. This is itself a market-positioning opportunity: a rigorously measured Bulgarian nightclub pilot would produce evidence none of the three incumbents currently have in public view for this exact venue type.
8. **Toast's public resource-center content is unusable for benchmarking** — 22 of 22 pages reviewed were lead-generation gates with zero disclosed figures. Do not cite Toast in a pitch deck for any quantitative claim; if Toast data is needed, it would have to come from its gated downloads or its separate "On the Line" article library, neither of which was in scope for this research.
