## A. Findings that need Martin's answer

### 1. The prepayment is a payment the platform has never made: no table, no POS, no receipt, and it is the first payment made from outside the venue (money, break): rows 2.27, 2.31, 2.34, 6.23, 7.20

**What the rows assume.** 2.27 holds the guest's card through Stripe and takes the money on Confirm (or at booking where the venue confirms automatically); 2.34 lets a hostess mark it paid in cash; 6.23 records a Dashboard refund against the reservation "with its storno to-do"; 7.20 marks the record paid "by itself when an online payment succeeds".

**What the platform has.** Every payment is a bill payment at a table: `payments.table_id` and `sitting_id` are NOT NULL (`packages/db/src/schema.ts:2652-2653`), `kind` is `on_bill` or `pay_first` (`:2778`), the route runs hold → payment table → lines moved → close → capture (D127 §The route; `PAYMENT_STATES`, `schema.ts:2541-2557`), and the storno to-do names the payment's table and the POS's receipt number (`packages/core/src/service/payment-todos.ts:31-40`; D122 §8). A prepayment has none of these: it is held at booking, captured on Confirm with no POS step at all, released on Decline. It is a third kind of payment with its own short route, and it must still live in `payments`: a refund notice is matched by the provider's id of the payment (`schema.ts:2766-2768`; `packages/payments/stripe/src/provider.ts:539-543` drops a payment that carries no id of ours), so a prepayment kept in a table of its own would make 6.23's "Stripe's notice reaches Restro" find nothing.

**The receipt.** D98 says "every capture records a receipt reference"; D122 §8 lets the e-shop route go because "a guest paying from inside the venue is not paying remotely"; D125 put "Q75 (deposits and song requests)" outside the pilot. The fiscal research reads the law as: a deposit paid by card needs a fiscal receipt from the venue's device when it is received (`2026-10-17-fiscal-rules-in-app-payments.md:74`), through the "ЧАСТИЧНИ ПЛАЩАНИЯ" department, with the value discount on the final bill (`:75-76`), and a deposit paid from home is still not "неприсъствено" because the service is used at the club (`:77`, lawyer question L4). Restro never draws a receipt (D127) and never does back-office work in a POS (rule 7). So at Confirm the venue must issue the receipt at its till by hand, and nobody has said so.

**A scene.** Thursday. Elena books Saturday's booth online, the club asks €100 up front, her card is held. Friday the hostess taps Confirm: €100 is captured. Nothing prints. Saturday the booth's bill is €640; the waiter rings it in full, the fiscal device issues a €640 receipt, and the €100 was never fiscalised. Three weeks later the owner refunds €100 in Stripe for a different guest's cancellation; the storno to-do asks for a receipt number that does not exist.

**Also decided without being said.** 2.31 lets "any hostess" confirm, and 2.27 makes Confirm the capture of a card. D122 §7 made "Receipt printed / No receipt" a manager's answer "because it decides whether a card is charged" (board Q4). Confirm charges a card from a PIN. And 2.27's "Stripe's 7-day hold" is not a fixed seven days: the provider declares 7 (`provider.ts:92`), but a Visa hold classed as merchant-initiated is 4 days 18 hours, and the real expiry is each payment's `capture_before` (`2026-10-22-stripe-knowledge.md:116`; `schema.ts:2707`), unknown for wallets (`:117`). "Nobody answering for 5 days warns the hostess" can warn after the money is already gone.

**Recommendation.** (1) Keep the prepayment, as a third kind `prepayment` in `payments` with no table, sitting or payment table, and a `reservation_id`; its states are requested → awaiting_guest → held → captured | released; the recovery job and the notices cover it like any payment. (2) On capture, a to-do for a manager or owner, like the storno to-do: "Issue the receipt at the till for Elena's prepayment, €100"; the receipt number is typed in (D122 §8's "staff never type it" is about the POS's own close, which this is not). Until the accountant answers Q75 and the lawyer L4, prepayment stays off in production; the row says so already, the write-up must say it as a switch Restro's team turns on per venue. (3) Confirm with a PIN stands (the guest asked to pay, the venue set the amount, the Log names the person), written as an exception to board Q4's line. (4) The warning is 48 hours before `capture_before`, not "5 days"; when the hold lapses the request stays, marked unpaid, and the guest gets a mail saying so. Amend D98, D122 §7 and §8, D125, D127.

### 2. A hostess cannot "sign in with her password", so 10.20 forbids what 2.23 allows (break): rows 2.23, 2.24, 10.20, 10.1

**The problem.** 10.20: "deleting a reservation or editing a past night needs her own password sign-in, as the settings already do". 2.23: "A hostess can delete the reservations she made". Staff work with a PIN and need no account (D122 §7 and §9; service-data log 4.6; `staff_members.user_id` is nullable, `schema.ts:2318`). A PIN actor is refused any settings-class permission before the database is asked (`packages/core/src/service/engine.ts:150-152`, `own_sign_in_required`; `permissions.ts:62-64` makes the class the `settings.` prefix), and a fresh sign-in needs a session's password time, which a PIN never has (`engine.ts:157-159`; `context.ts:171-181`). So under 10.20 a hostess without an account can never delete anything, and 2.23 is dead for her.

**Recommendation.** Deleting is an operational action: a PIN, the confirmation that names the party (2.23's own safeguard, and D107's one justified dialog), and the Log naming the person; a deleted reservation stays restorable, so the lower bar is safe. Editing a past night (4.25) keeps the password rule: it belongs to organisation owners, admins and Restro's team, who all have accounts. If Martin wants the password for deleting too, then every hostess who may delete needs an account linked to her staff row (`staff.link_account`, `commands.ts:629-639`), and 2.23's "hostess" means that.

### 3. What the night's end does to Requested, Waiting and blocks is undecided, and one row says the opposite of another (money, gap): rows 6.20, 2.27, 3.7, 5.20, 2.22

**The problem.** 6.20 ends the night's reservations by itself: "the seated become Finished, those who never came No-show". Nothing says what becomes of a **Requested** booking (online, unconfirmed) for a night that has passed, of a **Waiting** party (3.7 sent it to Feature 6, where no row answers), or of a **block** on that table (5.20 made a block "a reservation", so a literal close marks it No-show). 2.27 says a request "never cancels itself" and "the request stays open", so a Requested with a card hold outlives the night it asked for, holding a guest's money for a night that is gone until the hold lapses on its own.

**A scene.** Petya requests Friday's table online on Wednesday with €50 held; nobody answers. Saturday 07:00 the night ends: her request is still Requested, her €50 still held; on Tuesday the hold lapses and Stripe cancels it (`2026-10-22-stripe-knowledge.md:116`). Nobody wrote to her, and the list of requests shows a Friday that will never come.

**Recommendation.** At the night's end: Requested → Declined by the night's end, with the hold released and the Declined mail sent (2.35); Waiting → Not seated (a terminal status 2.22 lacks, or Finished with no table, shown apart); a block is untouched. 2.27's "never cancels itself" then means "never before its night has passed". Amend 2.22's list and D101.

### 4. A venue that confirms automatically and lets guests book without a table has no limit on online bookings (gap): rows 2.31, 2.32, 1.22

**The problem.** 2.31 lets a venue confirm automatically; 2.32 lets a venue take bookings with only night, time and party size; 1.22 makes seats warn and never refuse, and no row names a capacity for a night or a slot. A table-less booking holds no spot, so nothing stops the fiftieth party of twelve on a Saturday from being confirmed at once, and the hostess learns of it on the night.

**Recommendation.** Automatic confirmation applies only to a booking that holds a table the guest picked on the plan (the spot is then taken and the conflict check refuses the next one); a booking without a table always waits for the hostess. Where Martin wants automatic confirmation without tables, a cap per night in reservation settings is the least that works. Record it with 2.31.

### 5. A past date: 2.25 lets a hostess book it, 4.25 forbids her to touch it (break between rows): rows 2.25, 4.25, 6.22

**The problem.** 2.25 (10-31): "Dates are not restricted, in the past or the future". 4.25 (11-06): "Once a night has ended, only organisation owners and admins and Restro's admin team can edit its reservations". 6.22: "a booking added to a closed night warns". A hostess adding a booking on last Friday is either allowed with a warning (2.25, 6.22) or refused (4.25).

**Recommendation.** The later answer wins: a hostess books tonight and any future night; a night that has ended is 4.25's, with 6.22's warning for those who may. 2.25's "in the past" is struck. The lead may settle this as a consistency fix, but it changes a row Martin answered, so he should see it.

### 6. "Arrived is one tap" meets the PIN on a shared device (gap): rows 4.20, 3.21, 2.24

**The problem.** On a shared device every staff write asks the person's PIN, except the two quick answers Martin allowed on 10-26 (D122 §7; `packages/core/src/service/grants.ts:9-12`; `definition.ts:115-120`). This widens Martin's list of two, so it is his to say.

## B. Findings the lead can settle

### 8. "The service day's close" is not the 07:00 moment, and "End the night" must not be "Close all" (money, gap): rows 6.20, 6.21, 6.22, 6.24

**The problem.** `service_day.close` runs from an hourly worker tick (`apps/worker/src/jobs/service-day.ts:224`), only for an ended day that has sittings, sent orders or payments (`:58-69`), and is refused `payments_open` or `orders_open` while anything of that day is not final (`packages/core/src/service/service-day-close.ts:222-245`); its `service_days` row (`schema.ts:2275-2301`) exists only for such days, sometimes days later. So 6.20's "the service day's close (07:00 by default) ends the night's reservations by itself" would wait on a card hold a manager has not answered, and 6.22's "Restro's own service day already records it" is false for a night with reservations and no orders (every hand-made venue, every night the POS was paused).

Closing every table: "Close all" is `tables.close_all` (`commands.ts:398`) → `closeOpenTables(stx, 'staff_closed')` **without** `keepPaying` (`sittings.ts:472-475`); only the closing hour keeps a table whose payment is not settled (`closing-hour.ts:24`), because ending such a sitting "would kill a card form or lose the route of a held payment" (`sittings.ts:477-483`). An "End the night" built on Close all cuts a guest off mid-payment. And it is a Staff permission (`tables.open_close`, `permissions.ts:97`), while 6.24 makes ending the night an admin action.

"Names how many bills are still open": Restro reads a table's POS bill only while the table has an open sitting (`apps/worker/src/jobs/readings.ts:18`), and the floor reading carries no bill state (`packages/core/src/connectors/service/schemas.ts:168-178`). The number Restro can give is "tables open for phones whose last reading still showed lines", never the POS's open bills.

6.21: "A party still at its table keeps its code open" after a close is put back. Once a sitting ended, its passes are dead and its order emptied (`sittings.ts:210-247`); putting a reservation back can only open the table again as a new sitting, and the phones allow location once more.

**Recommendation.** (1) The night's end for reservations is its own worker step on the minute tick (`apps/worker/src/jobs/service-hours.ts:1-17` is where the closing hour lives), at the venue's service-day end (`venues.timezone`, `service_day_ends_at`, `schema.ts:76-78`), never tied to `service_day.close`; "a closed night" is a service date earlier than the venue's current one, computed, with no row. (2) `night.end` is a new command under its own permission (finding 13), which runs `closeOpenTables` with `keepPaying`, says which tables stayed open and why, and shows the preview D107 allows for an irreversible bulk action. (3) Word the count as "N tables still have a bill on the phones' side". (4) 6.21 reads "the table is opened again". Amend D122 §4 and §7.

### 9. There is no bill move in Tonight or in the connector, and a move made in the POS ends the sitting by itself (break, gap): rows 4.21, 4.22, 4.23

**The problem.** Tonight sends open, close, new guests, open all, close all, resume, mark payment table and the payment answers (`apps/web/src/tonight`, every `type:` it names); no move. The connector writes are `addLines`, `isolateLines`, `payAndClose`, `undoIsolate` (`packages/core/src/connectors/service/connector.ts:55-85`), and `moveLinesToTable` is declared for "the payment table of a one-bill POS" (`capabilities.ts:18-19`). D110 allows a move (`waiter-actions.ts:7-14`, `split_or_move_bill`), the interface does not have one yet, and Mistral question 11 (can the API move lines between tables; does it print again) is unanswered. So 4.22's "through Tonight as a waiter would" is a new connector operation, a new capability, a new staff command and a Mistral answer.

If staff move the bill in the POS instead, Restro cannot tell a move from a close: the old table's next complete reading shows the bill gone after it held lines and the sitting ends `bill_closed` (`readings.ts:298-304`), the passes die and the order is wiped; the new table has no sitting until someone opens it. 4.22's "Restro opens and closes the digital menus to match" works only for a move made from Restro.

4.23's swap of two seated parties with bills at a one-bill POS needs a third table in between; a payment table cannot be it (a payment owns its payment table, D127 3.11, and `openability` keeps them out, `sittings.ts:137`). 4.21 contradicts itself: "This is the reservation stage only" and "the new table's code opens"; a party not yet seated has no code to open, and a seated party moved before its first POS line loses its unsent order with the old sitting (`sittings.ts:215-232`).

**Recommendation.** A move after ordering is offered in Change tables only where the connector declares a new capability `moveBillToTable` (operation `moveBill`, declared `split_or_move_bill`); elsewhere the screen says "Move the bill in the POS, then tap Arrived on the new table" and the old sitting ends by itself. A move waits while a payment of the table is not settled (the `keepPaying` pattern, `sittings.ts:500-507`). A swap with two bills is two moves through a free table or "in the POS". Before the first POS line, a move closes the old code, opens the new one and carries the unsent order over (as 1.67 does for joining). Amend D110 (the operation), D122 §3 (the capability) and §4.

### 10. Online booking under rules 3 and 4: what it is allowed to read, write and show (break if built naïvely): rows 2.30, 2.32, 2.36, 5.1, 7.22, 2.34

**What the rules are in the code.** The guest page's loader follows the published files and nothing else (`apps/web/src/guest/load.ts:1-18`); what changes during the night comes through D121's two endpoints with the table code in a header, the per-code and per-phone limits, Turnstile on Send and Pay, and the forbidden-values walk on every answer before it leaves (`apps/web/src/server/guest-api.ts:3-6`; `packages/core/src/service/guest.ts:21-27`). A booking page has no table code: none of this serves it. It is a third public surface, and the rows do not say how it reads.

**What the model implies, and can honour.** (1) The still part is published like a snapshot: the plan's geometry, spot names, kinds and seats, which nights and hours the venue takes bookings, whether guests pick a table (2.32), the prepayment amount and wording (7.22), written by the publisher when the plan or the settings change. (2) Availability is live (a walk-in seated a minute ago, a request that just landed), so it is one public read endpoint answering booleans per spot per slot for one night, with a per-address limit and a short cache. (3) The request is one public write with Turnstile and the Send's limits, source `guest`, through the reservations write path (1.66). (4) What never leaves: table codes (a spot's link to a code, 1.40; a code is a secret, `schema.ts:1183-1195`), POS ids, other parties' reservation ids, names, counts of guests, a block's reason, the venue's internal notes; the guest's own booking is addressed by a random booking token whose hash is stored, like a table code, never the `rv_` id in an address, and the token is also what 2.34's payment link carries. The forbidden-values walk runs on every answer. (5) Embedding (2.30) needs a `frame-ancestors` per venue: the guest pages set script, frame and connect sources only (`apps/web/src/guest/headers.ts:104-131`); nothing in the app sets a frame policy today.

**Service hours twice.** 2.36 puts "working hours per weekday and closed dates" in reservation settings. The venue already has service hours per weekday with opening, last orders and closing (`packages/core/src/ordering-settings.ts:17-42`, stored under `venues.settings.ordering`, changed by `settings.ordering_set` under `settings.guards`, `commands.ts:509-521`), which close the tables (D122 §4). Two settings for "are we open on Monday" will disagree. Note also that a venue that never set its hours "is never gated" (`packages/core/src/service-hours.ts:89-92`, `:205`): the booking page would offer every night.

**Recommendation.** One source: the booking page reads the venue's service hours; reservation settings add only closed dates and a last-booking time. Online booking stays off until the venue's hours are set. Record (1)–(5) with 2.30, and amend D38 (a second guest channel without a QR), D121 (a third public surface with its own guards) and D84 (the address `/book/<organisation>/<venue>`).

### 11. The Log cannot hold "before and after" of a guest's details, has no reservation id, and its kinds are a closed list (break): rows 9.20, 9.1, 9.21, 2.23, 8.21

**The problem.** A Log entry's details are "flat facts with short keys. Never a note, never guest data" (`packages/core/src/service/log.ts:86-93`); the OWASP line the design followed keeps personal data out (`2026-10-20-service-log-design.md:22`); an entry names a sent order, a payment, a table or a sitting and nothing else (`log.ts:96-106`; `schema.ts:2069-2073` and the check at `:2092-2095`); every kind and its retention are fixed in `LOG_KINDS` (`log.ts:33-73`). 9.1's "before and after" (their RV-343) cannot carry a name, phone or email; "readable by reservation" needs a column; "per calendar day" (RV-344) is gone, our Log is by service day. 9.21 wants older nights "readable as records in the Log", so every reservation kind must be `record` (18 months); a `night` kind is purged after 31 days (`apps/worker/src/jobs/purge.ts:1097-1105`). 2.23's "until it is erased" names no term for a deleted reservation.

**Recommendation.** Reservation entries log the change, never the values of name, phone or email ("contact changed" is the whole entry); tables, hour, party size, status and the pay rule log before and after as codes; a `reservation_id` column joins the Log's id columns; the kinds are `record`. A deleted reservation keeps its row with `deleted_at` (app code deletes nothing), restorable for 30 days (D83's window) and then erased by the purge on its own `retain_until`. Tonight already lists 31 nights back (`apps/web/src/tonight/day-picker.tsx:18-19`), which is 9.21's month. Amend D122 §9.

### 12. "Erased by the purge job" is the wrong tool, and a guest has no identity to count visits by (gap): rows 8.21, 8.22, 8.20, 2.26

**The problem.** The purge job erases whole venues and rows past their retention (`purge.ts:407-542`, `:1020-1155`); nothing erases one person's details on request, and D83's rule is about DELETE. Erasing a guest is blanking his name, phone and email on his record and his reservations, an UPDATE the app role may make through the reservations write path, with a `record` Log entry that names no one. The name must also be nowhere else: not in the Log (finding 11), not in alerts or Telegram (D122 §7: "no guest data"), not in the night's export. 8.22 counts "how many times he has come" per venue, which needs one record per guest that reservations point at; the only key a club has is the phone number, and 2.26 lets a venue make it optional, so a phoneless booking belongs to no guest and counts nowhere. 8.20's "the manager also has a private list, by default" has two readings (one private list per manager, or one list all managers share).

**Recommendation.** A venue's guest record (`gs_`), matched by the normalised phone number, holding name, phone, email, the visit and no-show counts and the venue's note; a booking without a phone links to none. Erasure is a command (`guest.erase`, sign-in class, the manager's) that blanks the record and unlinks it; the purge job gains nothing. 8.20: one private list per manager account, since a shared "managers' list" is the venue's list with a smaller audience. Amend D83 (what erasure is) and D122 §9.
