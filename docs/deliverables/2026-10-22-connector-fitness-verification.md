# Verification of the connector-fitness fixes: how real each issue is, how robust each fix is

The lead proposed fixes for the gaps of `2026-10-21-connector-fitness-what-ifs.md` (round 1) and `-round-2.md`, and sorted them into "platform, settle before the payment route is coded", "platform, additive and cheap", "connector only" and "out by choice". Martin asked for two things of this review: for each issue, is it a real problem for a POS or provider a Bulgarian club or bar would run, or a theoretical one; and for each fix, would it hold under load and failure (a timeout, a waiter acting in between, two phones at once, a partial reading, a provider notice out of order). The four checks behind each grade are: correct against the decisions in force, platform or connector, minimal, hidden cost. Scenario numbers are the lead's chat numbering; the round-2 table's own numbers are given in brackets where they differ. Words follow `GLOSSARY.md`. "The isolated bill" means what the POS makes when lines are set apart for paying: a payment table, a split-off POS bill, or the POS bill itself.

Realness grades: **real** names the POS or provider that behaves so, from the research the two rounds cite; **plausible** is known elsewhere or stated without a source; **theoretical** has no known case.

## In short

Of the eighteen issues the lead's fixes answer, **8 are real, 8 are plausible and 2 are theoretical** for a Bulgarian venue. Of the eighteen fixes, **5 are robust as proposed, 9 are robust with one change and 4 are weak.** The lead's grading is mostly sound; it overstates two issues (the capture that happens asynchronously, 52, and the Square rule, 25, which no Bulgarian POS in the research shares) and understates one (a POS bill left open from the night before, 37, which at the pilot's one-bill POS decides where tonight's first sent order lands).

**The weakest fix is 17+31, "the hold is on the POS's total".** It holds only when the guest pays a whole POS bill. For "pay my items", the pilot's main case, the picks have no POS total until after the lines are moved, and choice B holds the card before any line moves. Restated (hold on Restro's sum for picks; the last check compares the isolated bill's POS total to the hold), it is robust and keeps both the #51 property "the amount captured equals the total closed with" and the minimum card amount. **The most wrongly classified fix is 25**: its fallback is connector-implemented but platform-declared and platform-tested, and it makes every whole-bill payment at such a POS a several-closes-under-one-hold case, which round 1 graded "fits" and the classification does not settle.

## The two specific claims

**"`isolateLines` returns the bill itself when the picks are a whole POS bill": safe for the last check on a many-bill POS?** Safe at the check, not race-free. The last check reads the isolated bill and refuses on any extra or missing line, so a waiter's round that lands before the read-back is caught. Two things differ from a payment table. Nobody writes to a payment table, but everyone keeps writing to a live POS bill, so the release-and-fee rate is higher. And Restro itself writes to it: D117 lets a Send run during a Pay, and D115 sends to the oldest open POS bill, which may be the bill being paid; a line that lands between the read-back and the close is closed with a total above the hold and captured for less than the POS recorded, with no POS reliably refusing a wrong amount (D112). The fix is the platform's (7+8): exclude a bill under payment from sends, and add "Restro's own sent order lands between read-back and close" to the model-based saga test. The undo step on the bill itself is a no-op, and a second close is refused because the bill is closed, so those two suite checks still hold.

**"The hold is on the POS's total": does it break "the amount captured equals the total closed with" or the minimum card amount (D112)?** Not for a whole POS bill: hold, close total and capture are the same number, enforced at the last check. For picks it cannot be applied at all, because the POS total of the isolated bill exists only after the move (17+31). With the corrected rule (hold on Restro's sum for picks; the last check compares the isolated bill's POS total to the hold; any difference is a mismatch), the property holds by construction, because a capture happens only when the close total equals the hold. The minimum card amount compares against the held amount in both cases, so it is unaffected.

## Missed or new problems

Only problems a proposed fix itself creates:

1. **Fix 7+8 with "the bill itself" as the isolated bill** opens the race above: Restro's own sent order (D117 allows a Send during a Pay; D115 sends to the oldest open POS bill) lands on the bill under payment between the read-back and the close. A payment table never had this, because nothing else writes to it.
2. **Fix 25's fallback** makes every whole-bill payment at such a POS several closes under one hold, which with `partialCapture` off has no clean recovery from a close that fails midway. The fallback turns a rare case into the normal one.
3. **Fix 23** keeps a write-capable provider key alive for 18 months after disconnect; a replayed capture or release job can act on the old account unless the platform guards it.
4. **Fix 22** puts the receipt email into a worker job payload, which pg-boss stores and archives in Postgres, so "never stored" is not true as designed.
5. **Fix 17+31 as stated** makes every "pay my items" on a POS with a bill-level charge hold the wrong amount, and turns a cent of rounding into a released hold and a myPOS cancellation fee per guest.
