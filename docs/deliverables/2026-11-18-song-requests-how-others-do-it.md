# Song requests: how comparable products do it

Research deliverable, 2026-11-18. Written for the lead, to be turned into options for Martin. It records what products say and charge; it recommends nothing (the one reading of the sources is in "The pattern").

## How to read this file

- **Read-dates.** `(11-18)` means the page was read on 2026-11-18, `(11-19)` on 2026-11-19. Pages were fetched through a fetch agent, so quotes are short and relayed; where the agent reported a model summary of a page rather than its text, that is stated.
- **Source grade.** *Primary* = the product's own pricing, terms, help, store listing or site. *Fallback* = a blog, forum, news item, review aggregator or vendor-written article, marked as such.
- **Marks.** "not found" = looked for, not stated on any page read. "inferred" = reasoned, not read. "search snippet only" = seen in a search result's summary, page not read (not to be relied on).
- **Currency.** Stated as the page states it. Most DJ apps print "$" with no code.
- **Scope.** No product found anywhere runs an open auction between guests. The closest cases are flagged in each section and in "The pattern".

## 1. Jukebox and request platforms

### 1.1 TouchTunes (US/Canada/UK bar jukebox)

1. **Charging model.** Credits bought in the app (primary: `https://touchtunes.helpshift.com/hc/en/8-touchtunes/faq/764-how-do-i-purchase-credits-1720565856/` 11-19). No dollar-per-credit figure on any TouchTunes page; the operator sets the price of a play. A **Fast Pass** moves a song into a priority queue for extra credits: the button "will show how quickly your song will play, and the amount of additional credits required" (`.../faq/766-what-is-fast-pass-and-how-does-it-work/` 11-19). **Dynamic Fast Pass** is a price the platform sets from queue length: one extra credit once the queue has "at least 10 songs total, or at least 5 Fast Pass songs", rising to "3 credits above the base Fast Pass price" at 20+ songs; "values used in the algorithm are managed by TouchTunes" (`https://touchtunes-tempo.helpshift.com/hc/en/4-tempo-us/faq/379-what-is-dynamic-fast-pass-how-does-it-work` 11-18, 11-19). The base Fast Pass surcharge is set by the venue "Location > Settings > Money" (same page). **Hyper Pass** jumps ahead of earlier Fast Passes, "only one song can be played using Hyper Pass at a time", with a timer before reuse at that location; price "number of credits required", no figure (`.../faq/1514-what-is-hyper-pass-1784903014/` 11-19). **Upgrade** turns a queued normal play into a Fast Pass; it plays before normal plays "but not before any Fast Pass songs that are already in the song queue" (`.../faq/1356-how-can-i-upgrade-a-song-in-the-queue` 11-18). Dollar figures exist only as user claims (fallback, user reviews, undated): "18 credits for $5, and is now 12 for the same price"; "50 + 2 bonus credits for $20" (`https://justuseapp.com/en/app/378351144/touchtunes/reviews` 11-19). The ToS says "The Company sets the cost of Credits" and it can change at any time; credits "are not money or legal tender" (`https://www.touchtunes.com/termsofservice`, last updated 2026-04-17, 11-19).
2. **Who controls what plays.** The platform plays from its catalogue through the venue's jukebox; the venue can skip. The ToS lists "venue electing to skip a song" among reasons a song may not play, and says the venue operator "will determine who can access and play songs" on unlimited jukeboxes (ToS §4.3, Exhibit A, 11-19). The help article on missing plays cites "the ability of location staff to control or switch the audio source" (`.../faq/758-why-didn-t-i-hear-my-song-play/` 11-19).
3. **Paid song never plays.** Kept. ToS §4.3 "No Refunds": "WE DO NOT OFFER REFUNDS FOR ANY PAYMENTS OR CREDIT PRESENTATIONS OR REDEMPTIONS FOR ANY REASON OR UNDER ANY CIRCUMSTANCES", with the parenthesis naming "IF YOU DID NOT HEAR YOUR SONG PLAY OR IT DID NOT PLAY" (read in full, 11-19). §9: the "sole and exclusive remedy" is to stop using the service; §10 disclaims liability for "DELAYS OR FAILURES TO PROVIDE THE SERVICES YOU HAVE REQUESTED (E.G., SONG PLAYS". Fallback (radio-station blog, 2022-01-30): a user's bartender skipped his song within a minute; he found no refund route (`https://kool1017.com/consider-this-when-using-a-touchtunes-jukebox/` 11-19). That blog quotes older, different wording ("PAY SERVICES UNDER ANY CIRCUMSTANCES") that is not in the 2026 terms.
4. **Guests see each other.** The queue "displays all currently selected songs at the Place you are checked-in to"; your songs get a badge, upgraded or Fast Pass songs are "marked in gold", a "Played" tab exists (`.../faq/795-what-is-the-song-queue-and-how-can-i-view-it/` 11-19). A user can play as "Anonymous", which implies names are otherwise shown (`.../faq/1342-why-do-my-songs-show-they-were-played-by-anonymous-1754317912/` 11-19); ToS §3.2: the "public profile name for music services" may be public. Amounts: not found. Leaderboard: not found.
5. **Several guests, one song.** not found.
6. **Repeat / cooldown.** not found on any page read (ToS, Community Guidelines, queue articles). Hyper Pass has a frequency limit (see 1).
7. **Receipts and tax.** Contracting party is "TouchTunes Music Company, LLC" (ToS preamble). Receipts: not found. "Taxes and other applicable fees may apply" (§4.1). Payment processor: not found. Residence limited to the US, Canada or UK (§2.1); no EEA terms (11-19).
8. **Licensing.** Catalogue: "We partner with different music labels" (`https://touchtunes.com/jukeboxes/experience-touchtunes` 11-18). Public-performance rights: not found in the ToS; the Company is stated to own the service components, and users uploading music must hold their own licence (§2.4.1).
9. **Abuse.** Obscenity and hate speech banned (§2.2); Community Guidelines (effective 2026-10-02) allow removal, suspension or blocking, appeal by email, "decisions are final" (`https://www.touchtunes.com/community-guidelines` 11-19). A flagged username may be switched to Anonymous (Anonymous article above). Chargebacks: §4.1 lets TouchTunes charge the user "any chargebacks charged to us" on a reversed authorisation (ToS, 11-19). Individual claims go to arbitration with a one-year filing limit (§13).

## 2. DJ-facing request and tip apps

### 2.1 Enjoy the Club (AppXpert Solutions SL, Málaga, Spain) — closest to the MVP's shape

Primary pages: `https://enjoytheclub.es/en/`, `/en/solutions/djs/`, `/en/solutions/venues/`, `/en/terms-and-conditions/`, `/en/faq/` (11-18).

1. **Charging.** Guests pay per request by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Revolut Pay or PayPal; "the price includes handling fees". Example amounts on the home page: a €2.50 request, a €5.00 tip. The DJ app is free; DJs "keep 90 %" of each song in their own space or at free-plan venues (the other 10 % is implied, not stated as a fee). Venues: 15-day trial, free plan, paid song packages "€25 a month for small venues and €50 a month for large venues". Where the venue sets the DJ's share, the DJ gets 90 % of that share.
2. **Who controls.** The DJ or venue approves or rejects each request ("Who decides what gets played?" answered "You do"). Closed catalogue and auto-expire options. DJ plays with their own software; the product "does not play music".
3. **Paid song never plays.** Money is held, not taken: "Guests are only charged if the song plays" (DJ FAQ); the card hold is voided and "no charge is made" if the song is not played (Terms §4.1). Disputes through a support ticket; refund at the company's discretion (§4.3).
4. **Guests see each other.** The screen shows a live queue and a "Most requested" vote list. Terms §4.1: waiting requests "are shown in order of amount", so the queue is ordered by money. Whether names or amounts are shown to other guests: not stated. Leaderboard of payers: not found.
5. **Several guests, one song.** Yes: "several people can chip in on the same song" (DJ FAQ); "Multiple users can contribute to the same request" (venues page).
6. **Repeat / cooldown.** not found.
7. **Receipts and tax.** The company is the seller to the guest and issues the receipt "even if the price is set by the venue or the DJ" (Terms §2.2); optional email for receipts (§3.2); the company then pays venues and DJs their share (§5.1). Guests must declare they are 18+ (§3.1).
8. **Licensing.** "We do not manage music licenses or copyrights. Each venue, DJ or organizer is responsible" (Terms §2.3).
9. **Abuse.** Prohibited-activity list and suspension right (Terms §6, §15.2); photos moderated by "automated AI-based moderation" (§8.6); DJ-side filters on explicit content and bans on songs, artists or genres (Google Play listing text via search, 11-18, search snippet only). Offensive request titles/names and chargebacks: not found.

## 3. Bulgaria and the EU

- **Apps.** VIBY (Bulgaria) and Rekwest (Netherlands) above; Enjoy the Club (Spain) in 2.1; Moodby and Tringbox in 1.5. No Bulgarian product with paid song requests, clear pricing and terms was found. VIBY is the only Bulgarian one and publishes no amounts.
- **EU consumer-rights wording.** VIBY and TipTunez listings both say EEA consumer-rights do not apply to their agreement with the provider (store listings, 11-19 / 11-18). Rekwest's terms mention a 14-day withdrawal right ending when supply starts with consent (11-19). Enjoy the Club makes itself the seller of record and the receipt issuer (2.1). Inferred: how the seller is described varies a lot inside the EU.
- **Chalga and pop-folk venues: the cash practice.** No source describes a current price list for paid greetings or song dedications; the question was searched in Bulgarian and English and the plain answer is that it is **not documented online**. What exists is context, all fallback (press, one sponsored travel guide):
  - 1990s: singers left venues with "торби с бакшиши" (bags of tips); a guest "вдига ръка и казва 'пак'... ако е достатъчно доволен - плаща" (raises a hand, says "again", and pays if pleased); "Попфолка винаги е слушал този, който плаща" (pop-folk was always listened to by whoever pays); a large part was cash; sums are "participants' memories", hard to verify (`https://www.168chasa.bg/article/23526093`, published 2026-09-12, 11-19; the related 24chasa piece `.../23526459`, 2026-09-09, is partly paywalled, 11-19).
  - Singer pay is a fee plus tips: one 2007 profile, "$10 a night at clubs, plus tips" (`https://homelands.org/stories/pop-singer/` 11-19).
  - Today: "Tipping the singer and table display can be part of the culture, so watch before you join in", with tips possibly needing cash (`https://europenightlife.com/bulgaria/sofia/best-clubs-in-sofia`, labelled sponsored, 2026-08-26, 11-19). Headline singers' club fees are reported as bank transfers with taxes included (search result from a Ruse news site, 11-18, not read); who keeps table tips today, and any price for a spoken greeting or a requested song: not found.
  - Read cautiously: the written sources say money goes to the performer in cash and that guests order a song by raising a hand; none says the venue or a DJ sells the order. That is an inference from silence, not a finding.
  - Not read: the 2018 Capital.bg piece "Животът ни е попфолк" (403).

## 5. The pattern (a reading of the sources, not a recommendation)

**What the industry has converged on.** Across the two families the money goes one of two ways. Jukebox services (TouchTunes, AMI, Roqbot-era Rockbot) sell credits and a priority tier: a fixed extra price, or in TouchTunes' case a price the platform raises as the queue lengthens, never a price one guest sets against another. The DJ and tip apps (Enjoy the Club, Rekwest, TipTunez, Special Request, the Requestify family, RequestSongs, DJ Requestify) let the DJ or venue set a minimum or a package price, and the human decides whether to play it; the guest's money is held or refunded if the request is declined, and in the two that say so (Enjoy the Club, TipTunez) it is also released if the song does not play. Several of the lowest-cost apps never touch the money at all: the guest tips the DJ through the DJ's own link. The venue-music subscription services (Rockbot, Soundtrack, Moodby, Splash) avoid the question by having the venue pay and the guest request free, from an approved library.

**Who controls the music and what happens to the money.** In almost every DJ app a human can say no, and the apps that say what happens next charge only on acceptance or on play. TouchTunes is the opposite case: the platform plays, bartenders can skip, and its terms keep the money. That is the loudest source of the complaints found. Licensing is almost always pushed to the venue or DJ (Enjoy the Club §2.3, Soundtrack, Rockbot's licence page, Tringbox); only Rockbot (for the US), PlayMySong (2013) and Soundtrack-type background-music services state that they supply licensed audio, and even those leave the venue's own performance licence in place. Receipts and the seller of record are rarely stated; Enjoy the Club, in Spain, is the one found that makes the platform the seller and issues the receipt. Cooldown rules are almost absent as a published rule: a host setting for the interval between a guest's requests (RequestBox), a per-person limit on open requests (RequestSongs), an operator "delay between plays" (AMI), a frequency limit on the top tier (TouchTunes Hyper Pass) and merging duplicates by voting (RequestDeck, DJ Requestify) are all that was found.

**Where an open auction sits.** An open auction (anyone can outbid anyone, the queue ordered by total money, several people chipping in, names and amounts public, paid greetings) was not found as such in any product. The nearest neighbours each cover part of it: Enjoy the Club orders waiting requests "in order of amount" and lets several people chip in on one song, with the charge taken only if it plays; TouchTunes lets money buy a place in line, but through platform-set tiers and with names (not amounts) visible; RequestSongs sells a "bump to the top" at the host's option. Paid greetings and shout-outs appear in only two small apps seen in snippets (TipTunez, Vibe Nexus); no larger product sells them. So, read from the sources, the MVP's auction is **alone** on public amounts and an open outbid, **between** on chip-in-and-order-by-money, and **common** on the held-then-charged-only-if-played payment and the human's veto. The chalga cash practice (a guest pays the performer directly for a song or greeting, in cash) is not documented as a price list anywhere; the written sources describe it as a performer-guest transaction, which is closer to the tip apps than to a venue-run queue.

## 6. Where the auction model is known to go wrong

What the sources say; much of it is anecdote, and no source measures any of it.

- **"A bidding war" with the house.** A TouchTunes user: "you are basically in a bidding war against the bartender" and "someone with more money comes along and skips the line?" (user reviews, undated, `https://justuseapp.com/en/app/378351144/touchtunes/reviews` 11-19). The platform's own response is the Hyper Pass limit and the capped Dynamic Fast Pass surcharge. A search-result summary of a Clarity report listed dynamic pricing among top complaints (secondary, not read).
- **A paid song that never plays, and no way back.** TouchTunes users ask "Can I get my credits back if my songs were skipped by the bartender?" and report songs that "not a single one EVER played" (same reviews, 11-19); the terms say no refund in these cases (1.1). TipTunez and Enjoy the Club exist partly to say the opposite in plain words.
- **Pay-to-play looks like a bribe.** DJ forum posts: "Basically, you're being bribed to play a request"; the app founder concedes "money-hungry DJs going overboard" and says DJs may turn requests down (`https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/opinions-new-app-to-turn-song-request-and-shout-outs-into-tip-for-djs-w-your-help/71731`, 2015–2016, forum, 11-19). A 2020 thread: a guest could "influence an entire night" with a $20 tip; a second-hand claim that headliners "have been pulled off sets in Vegas" for not playing what big spenders wanted (`https://ourdjtalk.com/djchat/digital-song-request-service-djs-get-paid.50365/latest`, 11-19, second-hand).
- **Repeat paying for one song.** The same 2020 thread imagines someone paying repeatedly for the same song and the DJ refusing to be bound; no product was found with a stated rule.
- **Drunk guests.** A 2010 essay: "a nostalgic drunk could ruin everyone else's night" because "any awful or annoying song is available for just an extra 50 cents" (`https://www.popmatters.com/133606-jukebox-justice-2496110687.html`, 2010, essay, 11-19); a 2015 forum poster warns of an "angry drunk" after a declined song; a 2013 article says Playmysong added host caps on how often one user can request (search snippet, not read). No source reports drunk-spending refunds or complaints about spending specifically.
- **Chargebacks in nightlife.** not found as a documented problem. The only chargeback rules found are contractual: TouchTunes may bill the user for chargebacks (ToS §4.1); Enjoy the Club's terms were read and say nothing about chargebacks.
- **A humorous version of the same pressure.** A UK DJ published a "fines" list for over-requested songs (£1,000 for "Mr Brightside") because requesters "don't take no for an answer" (Mixmag Asia, 2024-06-18, news, `https://mixmag.asia/read/dj-pressplay-introduces-fines-list-song-requests-mr-brightside-news` 11-19). No money was reported collected.
- **A vendor-side warning.** RequestDeck's blog (vendor, 2026-10-20) advises separate standard and paid-priority links so the DJ keeps queue control.
