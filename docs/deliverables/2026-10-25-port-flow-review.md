# Review: the Build › Port flow (`.claude/skills/restro-flow/port.md`, PR #121)

Made by: independent review subagent (Fable 5.1), read-only, 2026-10-25. For: the lead's request for a second opinion on the Port flow before T82/T83 use it. Informs, never binds (D114).

**Verdict: the skeleton is right (one inventory, row ids, verdicts, grilling split by who can answer, parity per feature set) and should stay; it does not yet deliver its own promise, because the inventory lists the MVP's *nouns* but never its *rules*, three stages have no done-criterion, and two hand-offs point at skills used outside what their text supports.**

Checked facts are marked **[fact]**; the rest is my opinion.

## Findings, most important first

**1. Should fix (the central gap): rules are not inventoried, so stage 3 sorts choices nobody listed.** Stage 2 "from the code" lists screens, routes, buttons, messages, tables, fields, services, settings, timers, roles. Stage 3 then sorts "every choice hidden in the code" into covered/technical/product. **[fact]** No stage produces the list of choices. Validation, defaults, limits, calculations, state transitions, ordering rules (D99's queue order, a reservation's status machine), what the MVP does when an outside service fails or answers late, and behaviour that lives in *data* (enum values, seed rows, email and message templates, config and env values, database constraints) have no row kind, so they cannot get a verdict and cannot block a spec. This is exactly Martin's "which decisions buried in the code get grilled". *Add to stage 2 a fourth list:* "**Rules and defaults:** every validation, default, limit, calculation, state transition, ordering rule, interval, retry and failure path, each with its file and line, and every value in data or config that drives behaviour. Done when every field, timer, service and status row names its rules or 'none'." Verdicts and the block-the-spec rule then apply to rule rows too.

**2. Should fix: the two halves of the inventory are never reconciled.** The code search and the browser walk each have a done-criterion, but nothing ties them: a route in the code list that the walk never reached (an admin-only page, a path behind a timer) slips through with a story missing. *Add to 2b:* "Done when every screen and route in the code list was reached in the walk or is marked unreachable with the reason." This is the traceability-matrix move comparable teams make; it is cheap here because both lists already have ids.

**3. Should fix: the spec hand-off breaks `to-spec`'s premise and §5's unbroken window.** **[fact]** `to-spec` says "Do NOT interview the user; just synthesize what you already know" from the conversation; SKILL.md §5 keeps "one unbroken window from grilling to tickets". Port.md runs the grilling once per product (stage 4) and the spec per feature set (stage 6), possibly sessions later. *Say the substitution explicitly in stage 6:* "`to-spec` writes from files, not from the conversation: the inventory, the decision log and the D-numbers written in stage 4; its user stories are the inventory's stories by id." That also removes a second, parallel story set and makes "a keep or rebuild row missing from every story blocks the spec" checkable: end the spec with "rows of this feature set: n, covered: n". The Fable pre-publish check (§4, last bullet) is the natural place to verify the count.

**4. Should fix: behaviour the decisions add has no rows, so the "single source of truth" is contradicted.** **[fact]** D99 adds a lock window, backers, holds and captures; D101 adds channels, deposits via D98, SMS as a paid option; the MVPs have none of these. Port.md's verdicts are keep/rebuild/drop on MVP rows only. *Add a fourth verdict or row kind, "**add** (from D<n>)", written in stage 3 from the D-numbers.* Otherwise stage 7's parity walk (keep and rebuild rows only) never covers the behaviour that matters most, and the inventory stops being the one list.

**5. Should fix: stage 7's parity cannot be a browser walk for half the rows, and the developer cannot sign off on something they cannot reach.** **[fact]** `audit-behaviour.md` step 4 walks stories in the built-in browser; a timer, job, email, data-field or rule row cannot be walked. **[fact]** No deploy is allowed (CLAUDE.md), so a developer outside has no preview to open; template.md step 3 shows how much work even a LAN address is. *Rewrite stage 7's first two sentences:* "A screen, button or message row is walked as `audit-behaviour.md` steps 3–5; every other row is proven by a named test, and the inventory records the test beside the row. For rule rows, the MVP's own outputs on a recorded input sequence are the test's fixture (a golden master: the queue order for a given sequence of requests, a reservation's status after each action). The MVP's developer confirms from the per-row screenshots and the fixture results Martin sends; Martin accepts on the local preview." This is what "characterization of legacy behaviour" means in practice, and it is the only mechanism that makes a miss permanent-proof rather than once-checked.

**6. Should fix: three stages have no done-criterion.** Stage 2b (fixed by finding 2), stage 5 (design) and stage 6 (build; it points at build.md, which has its own, acceptable). *Stage 5:* "Done when every operational screen of the feature set has its screen plan, each story on it carrying an inventory row id, and every guest row has its entry in `menu-template-functionality.md`." Also say where the stage-5 screen plan lives until the tickets exist (**[fact]** ship.md §1 makes it a section of the ticket, written when tickets are cut): "kept in the inventory beside its rows until `to-tickets` copies it into the ticket."

**7. Should fix: who decides a drop.** Stage 3 has the lead set every verdict. **[fact]** SKILL.md §3: "scope … go[es] to Martin". Dropping or changing a behaviour the MVP has, when no decision or rule forces it, is a scope choice. *Add to stage 3:* "a drop or rebuild not forced by a decision or a rule is a product row and goes to stage 4." Also clarify *rebuild differently* means the behaviour differs, since the looks differ on every row by D105/D123; otherwise every row is "rebuild".

**8. Should fix: intake says nothing about secrets, live data, prerequisites or a moving MVP.** The MVP folder will hold a `.env` with the developer's real keys (YouTube, a payment provider); the note should hand over test keys only and name the keys needed, Martin supplying them (`upkeep.md` → Human-only step). **[fact]** The tracker runs T85 and T84 before T82; name "the tracker's prerequisites closed" in stage 1's done line. Record at intake whether the MVP holds real records; if so, their migration is a feature set of its own (D101's tool is "a demo", so probably none, but the flow should say so once). After the pin, a change in the MVP is a new row the developer sends, never a silent re-pin. A dependency the MVP uses enters only through a ticket that names it (CLAUDE.md "Never" list) with its terms checked: the YouTube search D99 keeps is a commercial-use question (API terms) and a stop-and-ask, not a port detail.

**9. Nice to have: pointers and duplication.** Stage 3's "Fable where money or the data model is touched" restates a subset of D104's heavy zone (roles, concurrency, time are in it too) and SKILL.md §4 already governs the rung at every step; replace with "the heavy zone applies at every stage (D104, §4)". "Live payment keys (rule 7)": **[fact]** rule 7 covers the POS; cite D125 or rule 9 for payment keys. Stage 6 "steps 3–8" overlaps stage 7 with build.md step 7's acceptance; write "steps 3–6". Stage 5's "then `template.md`" is wrong for the song-request guest page: **[fact]** D105 leaves the DJ queue's place in templates open (Q78), and D100's table-less page is not a template; point at Q78 instead. `RV-`/`SR-` hard-codes two products; "a two-letter prefix per product" ages better. Negations to turn positive: "never copied in" → "read as a spec"; "Restro deletes nothing of theirs" → "the developer archives the MVP". Stage 2's `research` is model-invoked and writes a repo file **[fact]**, matching its SKILL.md; `to-questionnaire` and `grill-with-docs` are user-invoked and gated by the card **[fact]**; both uses are sound. Say when the card is shown: once at stage 1, then one per feature set at stage 5, since the flow spans weeks.

## What is fine

The intake pin, the row ids, the two-model gap hunt plus the developer's "what is missing", the recommended-default grilling that asks Martin only product rows, the tap baseline taken from the MVP, and the per-feature-set cadence foundation-first are all sound and match restro-flow's division of labour. The file is short (20 lines) and has no sprawl; the fixes above add perhaps eight lines.
